Jeff Blackburn ‘67 (pictured at the inaugural Lunar New Year event) has been appointed to the position of Director of Advancement. Jeff has a long association with Auckland Grammar School, firstly as a student in the late 1960s and then as a parent to sons Chris ‘03 and Jamie ‘05, and daughter Katherine.

After serving on the Old Boys Executive Jeff joined the School Board in 2007, and became Chair in 2009. It was on his watch that Headmaster Tim O’Connnor was appointed in 2012. Jeff retired from the Board in 2019 after 12 years of service.

Having green-lit the 150th capital project of the new library Te Ara Matauranga and the pool, Jeff remained actively involved with the fundraising for the new facilities. His prime focus as the Director of Advancement is securing 10 Clive Road by the end of 2026.

A good teacher has a profound influence on a young person and is borne out in the research John says. “Having better quality teachers is linked with lower absentee rates, higher quality of life indicators and better self-efficacy scores for students. Economists have also put a dollar value on it; apparently students with the best teachers will earn tens of thousands more, over a lifetime.”

John has loved his time at Grammar. “I have very mixed feelings about moving on. The School’s culture, the culture of high performance across so many domains and the amazing students are things that I have really appreciated here.”

John started at Grammar as a History teacher in 2012, and has also been Head of History, Head of Social Sciences, and Director of Teaching and Learning in his time at Mountain Road. Teaching and learning in the COVID 19 lockdown period came under his remit, and was “certainly a challenging time, in all kinds of ways. However, looking back the lockdowns helped sharpen our focus on what is important about teaching and about being in school, so they were useful in some ways.”

John knows the value of educators and wants to help create them. “Great teachers change the way we see the world. They help us think and do things we wouldn’t otherwise be able to.” Dr John Etty is the definition of that teacher, and the New Zealand education system will be strengthened by this next phase of his career.

Auckland Grammar School’s very own ‘artist in residence’, Art master Justin Boroughs, had a solo exhibition of his work last November at Johnathan Grant Gallery in Auckland.

It was Justin’s 26th solo exhibition of paintings - he has exhibited in numerous group shows over the years. Justin’s pieces are in public and private collections in New Zealand, most notably perhaps the Christchurch City Art Gallery, and around the world. Justin humbly suggests, “I’m lucky enough to have built up a lot of interest in my painting over the years, some of it from the Grammar community. I think people like the familiar subjects of my work and the way I paint them.”

Justin was a student at the Elam School of Fine Arts in Auckland in the 1970s, studying under Gretchen Albrecht, Don Binney and John Turner. It is ‘Otsukaresama Deshita’, or ‘thank you for your hard work’ to Dennis Helleur. After 37 years of teaching at Auckland Grammar School, Japanese teacher Dennis Helleur is retiring.

Dennis has taught under three Headmasters - Sir John Graham, John Morris, and Tim O’Connor. His initial plan was to stay two years before returning to Tokyo to continue teaching English as a Second Language. “However, the two years turned into 37 years in the blink of an eye because Grammar is such a great school to teach at.”

Dennis says he really appreciates the pursuit of excellence that the School encourages and celebrates. “The quality of academia and the arts has been amazing to observe, and I have also enjoyed the opportunity to meet the sons of past students in my time here.”

Europe he returned to New Zealand in 1981, where he began a dual career as a painter and an art teacher at Grammar.

Justin is one of only a small number of contemporary realist landscape painters. His works are characterised by fine draughtsmanship, meticulous technique, evocative use of light, and a focus on well-known New Zealand locations and landscapes. Justin has his own take on how he describes his style. “People use the word romantic, but a lot say my work is semi-surrealist. These are places people go and know, but somehow I’ve done something slightly different…I don’t see much point in copying the landscape exactly as it is. You could take a photograph there and save a lot of time and trouble. But a photograph never, ever, ever gives you a true idea of the landscape.”

How fortunate the boys of Auckland Grammar School have been for the past 40 years to have a Justin Boroughs teaching them art.
