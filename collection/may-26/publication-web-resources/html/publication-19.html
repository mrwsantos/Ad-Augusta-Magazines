faculty PROFILE

Barbara McGowan, Head of Faculty Science answers some questions about a faculty she is hugely passionate about.

Firstly, what is your personal background with science?

I completed a BSc in Molecular Biology and Genetics and a BA (Hons) in Sociology at Victoria University of Wellington. The best thing about a conjoint degree programme is the range of papers you get to take across multiple faculties. It means that I was exposed to different ways of approaching knowledge and gathering information to form conclusions.

I then did a teaching diploma at Auckland College of Education, focusing on Science and Biology. Here at Auckland Grammar School, I mostly teach Biology, Junior Science and Earth and Space Science. I have also taught Chemistry at AS level and Pre-Q Physics. It’s good to teach across the sciences; learning chemistry and physics again has reminded me of what it’s like to be a student and it has also improved the way I teach my core subjects.

Are other science teachers as passionate as you?

Yes! The Science faculty is amazing to work with. It is unusual to get to work with so many people who are passionate about science and encouraging students to study it at a higher level. They are the reason why science subjects are so popular at Grammar. It has been a highlight of my career to be in a position where I get to work with the amazing people in my faculty and to see how they strive to instil a passion for science into every student at the School. The faculty is large; we have 33 full-time

science teachers, two teachers who teach science and another subject, two teacher trainees, and five technical staff. Science is strong here, it is almost like teaching in a science academy!

What is the Science pathway and what do students learn?

All students at Grammar study Science in Forms 3 and 4. The course gives them a strong foundation for senior science study.

In Form 5, there are a range of science options, as well as Triple Science where students take Biology, Chemistry and Physics in two option lines so they can then take an extra subject at Pre-Q. Science is compulsory for students in the bespoke NCEA Level 1 programme. Most students can choose their favourites from Biology, Chemistry and Physics to study. Students in 5I to 5O can also take Pre-Q General Science, which leads to NCEA sciences.

In Forms 6 and 7, students can choose Biology, Chemistry and Physics in the Cambridge and NCEA pathways. NCEA students can also study Earth and Space Science. The most capable students take scholarship examinations, and join Olympiad teams and the NZYPT (New Zealand Young Physicists’ Tournament).

Biology - the living world

Biology explores the diversity of life and how organisms interact with their environments. Students study life processes, evolution, and ecology, with a special emphasis on our unique ecosystems. By understanding human impact on nature, they learn to make informed decisions around sustainability and biological issues.

Chemistry - the material world

Chemistry investigates the composition of matter and the energy involved in its transformations. Students learn to interpret the world through the behaviour of atoms, molecules, and ions, using scientific symbols and conventions. This knowledge helps them tackle global challenges like environmental sustainability and the development of new medicines and materials.

Physics - the physical world

This strand explains phenomena such as light, sound, electricity, and motion through the central concept of energy transformation. By understanding how the physical world interacts and how these forces are represented, students can better engage with contemporary technological solutions and scientific challenges.

Earth and Space Science - planet earth and beyond

Students explore the interconnecting systems of the Earth and the wider universe. By studying how the geosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere, and biosphere depend on one another, students realise our role as guardians of finite resources. This understanding empowers them to address issues facing our planet and protect its future.

How well do Grammar students do?

Over 90% of students choose to continue with at least one science in Form 5 and while some students may choose not to do so, they finish the junior programme with a good foundational knowledge that prepares them to be informed citizens when they are adults.

Its pleasing to see how many students choose to study at least one science throughout their time at Auckland Grammar School.

The science faculty consistently produces good results across its senior courses, especially when you consider the range of students who take at least one science subject. Rather than being limited to the top Cambridge and NCEA classes, students across all form classes are taking and succeeding in the sciences. Many students also look to pursue careers in science, and it’s pleasing to see that a significant proportion go on to either trades or tertiary study with a science focus.

When it comes to external examinations, science students excel. In the 2025 Cambridge pathway for A Levels, an A - C grade was gained by 85% of Chemistry students, 83% of Physics students and 77 % of Biology students studying A2.

NCEA Level 3 achievement standards were gained by 78% (Biology), 77% (Chemistry), 64% (Earth and Space) and 58% (Physics) of students.

Additionally, scholarships were awarded to 27 (Chemistry), 22 (Physics), 13 (Biology) and two (Earth and Space) students.

Why study the sciences?

The sciences encourage you to look at the world around you and question how it works, to look at things more closely and to step back and see human endeavours occurring within a much broader lens of how the living and non-living world interact. Given the increasing role of science in addressing big issues like climate change, it’s important that everyone has a strong foundation in scientific concepts so they can make informed decisions.

It also helps in everyday situations, like knowing why leaving the car door light on can drain the battery, or why it’s a good idea not to leave food uncovered on a table at a family get-together. Science has a lot going for it, and at Grammar there’s great support to do well in it, with a dedicated faculty of staff.
