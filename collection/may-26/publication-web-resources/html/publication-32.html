Sport has long been central to life at Auckland Grammar School, shaping not only performance but character. For Finn Goodson ’20, that foundation has led him to Wesleyan University, where he is combining high-level sport with a broad academic journey.

Finn credits the School’s philosophy of exploration for guiding his path. “Auckland Grammar School teaches you to explore before you specialise,” he reflects. It was not until his final year, through Cambridge AS Level Sport and Physical Education, that he began to see the sports industry as a genuine career pathway.

Finn is making the most of Wesleyan’s liberal arts model, working towards an Economics major while studying other subjects of interest, including Psychology and Computer Science. “It has given me a more complete understanding of Economics, seeing how it overlaps with other disciplines.”

Leadership and resilience were defining features of his time at Grammar. As Captain of Squash in 2024 and the recipient of the Hal Radford Memorial Trophy, he led the team to an Auckland Premier Championship title and a podium finish at Nationals. A comeback victory against Westlake Boys’ High School, from two sets down, proved to be a turning point. “It certainly reinforced the importance of mental preparation.”

Academically, Finn achieved Top in the World in AS Sport and Physical Education in 2024, an accolade that came very much as a surprise. “It had been a subject I looked forward to daily, but it was not one for which I expected an award. My initial reaction was one of shock mixed with confusion and gratitude.”

Now competing in the United States, he continues to develop in one of the world’s most competitive squash environments, embracing both its individual demands and strong team ethos.

Grounded in the values of Auckland Grammar School, Finn’s journey reflects a commitment to growth, resilience, and excellence beyond the School gates.

david radford ‘67

For Old Boy David Radford ‘67, Auckland Grammar School was not the starting point of a clearly defined academic pathway, but rather the place where one gradually took shape, with a deep intellectual curiosity that would ultimately lead him to the forefront of international nuclear physics research.

By his own account, he was ‘a very mediocre student’ until Form 5, when a growing interest in Mathematics and Science, combined with the support of inspiring masters, helped him find his stride. By Form 7, the opportunity to choose teachers for certain subjects proved pivotal, allowing him to excel in waysthat surprised even himself.

David knew from the age of 10 that he wanted to be a scientist, initially experimenting with a home-made chemistry set before discovering a passion for Physics. “Perhaps it was wanting to understand how things work at a fundamental level that drewme to it. I just loved it.”

David completed a PhD at the University of Auckland, before pursuing postdoctoral research overseas. Now based at Oak Ridge National Laboratory in Tennessee, he specialises in nuclear physics, particularly the development of advanced germanium detectors used to study atomic nuclei.

Today, David serves as Director of the LEGEND-1000 experiment, a major international collaboration investigating one of physics’ greatest unanswered questions: the nature of the neutrino. The project brings together over 300 scientists worldwide and could transform our understanding of the universe.

Despite his achievements, David remains driven by curiosity and a love of problem-solving. Reflecting on his journey, he credits Auckland Grammar School with shaping his path, praising its teachers and opportunities. His advice to students is simple: pursue your passions wholeheartedly - scientific discovery is both challenging and deeply rewarding.

When Graham Coop ‘78 first met Sir John Graham, it was as an out-of-zone student hoping to join the School. The Headmaster, looking up from Graham’s intermediate school report, asked “Why the poor social studies mark?” Despite this remark, Graham was accepted into the School allowing him to continue the Grammar legacy of his father and grandfather alongside younger brothers Andrew ‘81 and Philip ‘84, who followed Graham to Grammar. Graham came to the School with a strong interest in Maths and Sciences and pursued this intently, but was also able to explore his passion for classical music, under the leadership of Messrs Jack East and Ray Wilson.

After obtaining a Science and Law degree followed by three years’ legal practice with an Auckland law firm, Graham ventured to France, an adventure that was to last a decade. He quickly found a position at the Paris office of global law firm Freshfields, advising French clients in French on complex legal and economic aspects of price revision disputes. Through a catch-up with fellow Old Boy and friend Mark Oremland ‘78, Graham met a lovely French lady at a concert for New Zealand expats. The concert turned into a drink, and three years later, Graham and Géraldine were married in Paris.

Graham emphasises the values Grammar instilled in him as a reason for his success. “It gives you authority and conviction to have studied a subject rigorously, to follow this regimen with discipline.” He has also given back to the School in many ways, being Chair of the UK Friends of Auckland Grammar Board and helping organise many events in the UK.

Perhaps this idea of service and strength of character could be distilled into a simple couple of lines that the late Ian MacKinlay would add to the School Prayer whenever he was standing in lieu of Sir John Graham. They have stuck with Graham ever since his School days and represent the Grammar spirit perfectly.

LeLei LeLAULU ‘59

Lelei LeLaulu ‘59 showing Head Prefect Nico Stanley his name on the donations wall outside Te Ara before he was shown around the School.

When Lelei LeLaulu ‘59 stepped back inside the Great Hall, he had not been back to the School since he wore the uniform, “apart from watching a couple of 1st XV games in the 1980s.” A former 1st XV player himself, it was apt he was shown around the campus by current 1st XV player Nico Stanley. Lelei was pleased to see Te Ara so well utilised, a capital project he donated to.

Born in Samoa, and now living in Washington D.C., Lelei also enjoyed talking to a group of Pasifika students about his eclectic career, culminating in being one of 338 candidates nominated for the 2025 Nobel Peace Prize. It highlighted his groundbreaking contributions to sustainable development, tourism and global peace. Lelei discussed with students his long-held belief that tourism - the largest voluntary transfer of resources from the haves to the have nots in history - is a transformative force for economic empowerment and cultural understanding, with the potential to alleviate poverty and reduce global conflict. “I frame tourism as an ‘industry of peace,’ because human connection can bridge divides and foster empathy between cultures.” When asked for advice, Lelei suggested working hard, being open minded and staying curious as good traits to foster.

Lelei is described as a ‘development entrepreneur’ working at the confluence of climate change, tourism, food security and renewable energy. His leadership extends across numerous global initiatives, including advisory roles with the World Bank, the International Finance Corporation, Caribbean Media Exchange, (CMEx), and the United Nations, as well as chairing organizations such as the Earth Council Alliance and Foundation of the Peoples of the South Pacific International (FSPI) in the Pacific.

The world’s oceans are a key focus for Lelei. He is hoping to gain personhood status for them, just as the Whanganui River has. “By giving our oceans personhood, people will have to think twice about messing with them given they would have legal status.”
