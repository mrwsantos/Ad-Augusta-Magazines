David (DAVE) william Savidan ‘80

David passed away on 20 February 2026, aged 58, after sustaining extensive injuries from a cycling accident.

A second-generation Grammar student, his sporting talent was evident particularly in long-distance athletics. He represented the School in 1st X1 Football and Athletics at both Provincial and National levels in 800m, 1500m and cross-country competitions.

After Grammar, David attended Auckland University, graduating with a Bachelor of Commerce and a Post Graduate Diploma in Commerce. He was awarded a University Blue in Athletics after gaining a Bronze Medal in the 800m at the Oceania Games overseas.

He then assumed sales and marketing roles at DB Breweries (NZ), Sara Lee (UK), and LVMH Watch & Jewellery (UK/USA). When returning home he formed his own company specialising in consultancy for sales growth, marketing transformation, and brand leadership. In 2022, Dave co-founded a biochar and carbon removal enterprise, ‘The Green Circle’.

As a passionate road cyclist, Dave and his cycling community combined this pursuit with raising money for charitable causes such as the Westpac Rescue Helicopter and youth suicide prevention for Covert Theatre. Last year he also completed a 500km ride in Fiji for Cure Kids to help fund oxygen units for remote Fijian areas.

Christopher (Chris) Barfoot ‘44

Chris passed away on 31 October 2025, aged 95.

At Auckland Grammar School, Chris excelled in English, topping the subject in his final year. This love of things literary led him to pursue a Bachelor of Arts at the University of Auckland, graduating in 1952. He then went on to attend the University of Oxford in England, completing a Master of Arts in 1956.

With his father Val Barfoot being a co-founder of Barfoot & Thompson with his colleague Maurice Thompson, the then well-established family business in real estate soon beckoned. Chris’ career in real estate would span 50 years with various roles both within the company itself and in the wider industry. He was a well-known face in Remuera, the local branch under his leadership being the top office in Barfoot & Thompson for six years prior to his retirement in 1998.

With some more time on his hands, the environment became a keen focus for Chris. He helped establish the Tahuna Torea Nature Reserve at Glendowie, where he is pictured above with Deputy Mayor of Auckland, Desley Simpson. The work began back in 1972 when the council proposed using the area for landfill. Chris and fellow neighbours connected with the Tamaki Estuary Protection Society (TEPS) took steps which resulted in a collective suggestion to authorities that the area be designated a nature reserve instead. With overwhelming public support, the plan was accepted.

In 1973, Chris became chairman of TEPS, a position he held until 1988, remaining on the committee until 2020. The Tahuna Torea Nature Reserve was just the beginning for Chris. He also campaigned for other nature reserves along the banks of the estuary, including Wai-O-Taiki Nature Reserve and Point England Reserve. He was aptly recognised ‘for a lifetime of championing the environment’ when he received the Orākei Local Board’s 2022 Environment Award.

Chris received the New Zealand 1990 Commemoration Medal for services to the environment and a Queen’s Service Medal in 2015 for services to the environment and the community. He was also awarded the Auckland City Good Citizen Award in 1989 and the Auckland City Millennium Medallion in 2000.

Ronald (Ron) Ian Chadwick ‘47

Ron passed away on 21 October 2025, aged 92.

In an interview for the School’s Headmaster’s Heritage Committee in 2021, Ron said his first day at Grammar left him with a feeling “that it was a fair place that provided inspiration to succeed”. That sense of equity and justice and a mindset to do your best was something he adopted. It served him well in his career in the New Zealand Police Force, and while at Grammar. Ron broke both school and inter-school records for discus, setting a record that stood for 29 years.

After initially working in the forestry industry, Ron joined the Police in Auckland’s Criminal Investigation Branch (CIB) as a 24-year-old. He was soon a detective, and later an inspector, moving from Auckland to Hamilton and Wellington before coming back to Auckland in the mid seventies to take up the role of Auckland’s Chief Inspector. In 1979 he was involved in the Erebus recovery mission, later being recognised in 2006 with the New Zealand Special Service Medal (Erebus) for that work. In 1981 his tasking included policing of the infamous Springbok match in Hamilton. It left a lasting impression on him. “Before the tour, I thought sporting contacts could help to change the political situation in South Africa, but I changed that view during the tour.” Ron retired from the Police as a Detective Superintendent in 1988.

Fagamoa (Fale) Su’e ‘84

Fale passed away on 16 January 2026, aged 55.

Fale was a champion hurdler in Form 3 at Auckland Grammar School, winning the 100m at the North Island Athletic Championships. He was also a talented rugby player who went on to become an integral part of Ponsonby Rugby Club’s success before representing the Pacific Barbarians in 1994.

The United States soon beckoned for Fale, and he played for Des Moines in Iowa from 1997-1999 before returning to New Zealand in 2000 to begin his coaching career at Ponsonby.

In 2010 Fale was injured in a freak accident that permanently consigned him to a wheelchair. Undeterred, he remained a popular coach for the Point Chevalier Pirates, Avondale College and the Glenora Premier side.
