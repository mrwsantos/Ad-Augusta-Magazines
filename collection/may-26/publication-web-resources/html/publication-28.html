Congratulations are extended to Stewart Germann ‘65, who was named as an Officer of the New Zealand Order of Merit in the 2026 New Year Honours for services to franchise law.

Stewart has made an extensive contribution to franchise law internationally. With more than four decades of experience developing franchise law in New Zealand, he is New Zealand’s only lawyer qualified as a Certified Franchise Executive.

Stewart has contributed to the academic development of international franchise law, publishing in peer-reviewed journals and legal textbooks, and lecturing at numerous tertiary institutions, including the University of Auckland, Auckland University of Technology and the University of Otago. He was the New Zealand representative for the World Franchise Council and the International Council of Chartered Secretaries and Administrators, and the Governor of the Australian and New Zealand College of Notaries and a past President of the Auckland Rotary Club.

As well as running his own successful law firm, Stewart Germann Law Office (SGL), which he founded in 1993, Stewart has served in many volunteer and paid leadership and governance roles, including as a Notary Public and with the Stroke Foundation of New Zealand, the Cognition Education Trust, the World Franchise Council and the New Zealand Middle East Business Council.

The inaugural 1st XI vs Old Boys Football match was held in the Term 1 holidays, an idea that was born with 1st XI Head Coach, David Warburton. An American with no historical ties to the School, David has coached the side for three years and has come to appreciate the depth of Old Boy connection and proud history within the 1st XI Football programme. He was keen to add this event to the season’s schedule to help strengthen community ties and provide an opportunity for current players to compete against Old Boys.

Despite a slow start with registrations and multiple injuries, an 18-strong side took the field against the current 1st XI team, with Old Boys from the 1990s through to leavers from the class of 2021 represented. Both teams came together around the centre circle prior to the start of the match, where David explained its significance and the hope that it would become a regular fixture for the 1st XI team.

With onlookers supporting both sides from the sidelines on the No. 3 field, the 1st XI team created the early running, but it was Old Boy Josh Stephan ‘16 who opened the scoring by capitalising on an error at the back by the 1st XI and emphatically struck home the opening goal.

This early goal rattled the 1st XI, who then found themselves being forced into errors by their more experienced opposition.

Any promising attacking forays by the 1st XI were snuffed out by Man of the Match goalkeeper Tim Lightbourne ‘89, who was a defensive superstar, and the Old Boys took a 1-0 lead into halftime.

The second half was a different story, as the 1st XI used their youth and fitness to their advantage, scoring early in the half through Ceejay Capate, before Theo Bond, whose father Stephen ’90 was captain of the Old Boys team, scored the go-ahead goal. Ollie Thompson then further extended the lead with a brilliant solo goal. Recent leaver Josh King ’21 scored a late goal for the Old Boys, but it was ‘too little, too late’ as it was the 2026 1st XI team running out eventual winners with the final score 3-2.

Tim Dale-Fuller ‘97 has spent a lot of time in the past six months thinking about the 1905 ‘Originals’ captain Dave Gallaher. The culmination of all that thought has come together in a documentary called From Field to Front, which played on TVNZ on Anzac Day 2026 and is now available on TVNZ+.

Made in association with the Auckland RSA, the documentary follows a soil exchange to and from Eden Park to places of significance to Dave Gallaher - where he was born in the Republic of Ireland (1873), where he played his last All Black test in France (1906) and where he is buried in Belgium, as a 1917 casualty of the First World War. Tim, together with fellow Old Boy James Gemmell ‘94 made a documentary in 2017, Beneath the Black, that included the Gallaher story as part of a wider look at rugby in New Zealand. Tim has valued looking further into Dave’s story. “It has been good to eXplore what led to Dave becoming the leader he was, and to see the people and places that helped shape him.”

The producer of the documentary is Jude Dobson, who works part-time at Auckland Grammar School as the Publications Manager. She says the act of exchanging soils, which is at the heart of the story, was emotive. “Whenua brings with it its own spirit or wairua, and working with Ngāti Whātua Ōrākei on this journey to exchange soil across the globe has been superb.”

James is the Executive Producer for the documentary. Timsays they both enjoyed the opportunity to delve into the history of the black jersey and work with Sean Fitzpatrick again, who presents the documentary. “Fitzy truly gets the history of the jersey and although James didn’t get to travel on this one, it’s been great to make another doco with him and have his focuson it back here in the edit suite.“

Jude is optimistic the production team’s efforts will strike a chord with viewers. “It’s been quite the journey, but our team has got there, and we hope people are moved by what they watch.”

Philip Stover ‘64

One year at Auckland Grammar School it seems is enough to make a life-long impression on a man. When Philip Stover’s father took up a Fulbright Lectureship at the University of Auckland in 1966, his son was enrolled at Auckland Grammar and his daughters at Epsom Girls’ Grammar School. Philip was in Form 5, with Henry Cooper as the headmaster. Nicknamed ‘the yank’, he was welcomed into the School community .”School uniforms were convenient and it really was a great year for me. I learned how to take notes, which was painstaking, but it was good for me when I got back to the States with further education.” The wider context of living in New Zealand also brought with it new learning. I learned how to enjoy tea, and the difference between mutton, hogget and lamb.” While signing up for basketball was an easy pick for an American teenager, Philip decided his New Zealand education should also include joining a school Cricket team.

The beauty of Māori carving inspired him to learn the craft. “I bought some recycled kauri from a shop called The Mill run by Mr Palmer in downtown Auckland and some carving tools.” The resulting pieces went back to the U.S with him when his year finished, and are proudly framed in his ‘New Zealand corner’ along with a block of kauri. He had intended to add a carved New Zealand chess set to his Kiwi collection, “ but I never finished it.”

After completing a BA in History with honours at McPherson College in Kansas, he graduated in-state with a J.D. law degree from Washburn University School of Law. Now retired, his work life included 12 years as the state’s attorney for his county, and 28 years defending criminal adults, as well being the court-appointed advocate in child welfare cases, and working in general law.

Philip has always engaged in Kiwi sport on the international stage. “I follow New Zealanders at the Olympics, and love watching the All Blacks and Black Ferns.” It appears just one year at Grammar has morphed into decades of connection to New Zealand.
