The vision of a Grammar precinct

“Job two”, Tim went on to explain, “is to develop the detailed vision - an aspiration for a new Grammar precinct. That is, the dream use of Tibbs House land and buildings, 8 Clive Rd land and residence and its neighbour 10 Clive Road.” Between them, No. 8 and, No.10 equate to 3000 square metres of land, which is a sizeable portion of land in central Auckland. The proximity of Tibbs House provides possibilities when the three properties are viewed collectively.

Tim describes ‘a Grammar precinct’ as the most apt terminology for the amenity that 10 Clive Road would add. “Going forward, roll size or demand for places at Grammar will not diminish. Our roll is at its largest in our history, at almost 2800. This year we have a cohort of 564 in Form 3, a 5th Form of 608 students and a 7th Form with 490. We are in unprecedented times.” The growing roll means more teachers, more students and more boarders.

Staff housing

Staff wanting to live in the Grammar zone to ensure they are within a reasonable commute to work are committing to high housing costs, which Tim spells out is challenging on a teacher’s salary. “Going forward, I can’t see that the cost of living in the Grammar Zone will decrease. Living in Auckland outside the zone is also expensive, and there is no ‘Auckland allowance’ for a teacher - they are paid the same as a teacher in Invercargill.”

While the School’s charitable entity in the Foundation Trust offers some extra

financial reward through the (competitive) Staff Awards at year-end and a modest donation towards the time spent in extra-curricular support of students, it is the possibility of subsidised housing that is hugely helpful to Grammar teachers.

Tim hopes that a Grammar precinct could provide townhouses for teachers at the 10 Clive Road site and through the back of Barnett Crescent, creating much more staff housing in the available footprint of land owned by the School.

The need is evident with an ever-growing waiting list for the 17 rent-subsidised staff houses on Barnett Crescent and along the northern side of Clive Road. Appropriate housing is not only an excellent way to attract and retain good teachers to teach at Grammar. Their proximity to campus enables them to be closely involved in the day-to-day life of the School.

Tim paints the picture of what a typical morning looks like on the Grammar campus with staff involvement in activities well before the assembly bell rings.

“In this past week, if you walked across the campus, before 7am you would have seen the likes of Mr Adam and Mr Trott leading cricket practices, and Dr Etty, Ms Greenslade, Ms Gaspar and Mr Lowe leading the ‘Learn to Row’ programme. You would have seen Mr Askew and Mr Rainger running members of the 1st XV while Mr Du Sart had others in the Weights Room, and you would have seen Mr Oates preparing boys for the Auckland Championships.That’s all before you arrive at Te Ara Mātauranga or onto the

B Floor for Maths tuition.” Tim strongly feels that having more staff housing will be a fundamental requirement for the School going forward. “If we are to retain the essence of Grammar, we need more passionate teachers living close by, so their job is achievable and rewarding.”

Tibbs House

A Grammar precinct also involves the development of boarding:

The current capacity for Tibbs House is 124 boys, but as Tim muses, “What is the ideal number for Tibbs? What if we could develop two separate boarding establishments on campus, each with 90 students, and they share a commondining hall?”

Then there are the questions about how best to prepare Form 7 boarders for the next phase of their lives. “What if we could provide our Form 7 boarders with flat-style accommodation so we can prepare them even better for independent living? What if we could have each of our boarders leave knowing how to cook five basic meals?”

Providing better spaces and amenities for boarders is not the only item on the wish list for Tibbs House. The accommodation for boarding masters needs to be addressed in any expansion plans.

A legacy that leads the way

Whatever the precise use of 10 Clive Road eventually is, it is the wider use of the various land holdings - either contiguous or proximal - that is important. Grammar has always strived for excellence in any endeavour, and, as Tim reflects, the 10 Clive Road legacy project is no different.

“Why could we not lead the way? Why would we not create a Grammar precinct that attracts engaged and committed students and skilled, passionate practitioners (and their families) who are truly rewarded for the surplus value they provide to generations of Grammar boys? No. 10 symbolises our aspirations for the future of Grammar. If we are to protect the essence of a Grammar education in 10, 20 and 50 years’ time, we need to begin

accepting responsibility for this now.”

The concept of ‘feeding forward’ is not new to the Auckland Grammar School community. For over 150 years the community of the day has supported the School to flourish not just for the time period they are in, but for future Grammar communities.

Tim’s timeline to the assembled crowd on the back lawn at No.10 encompassed a sizeable list of changes to the campus, which have often been supported financially by the community.

“From the act of moving from Symonds Street to Mountain Road, the international architecture competition to design our main building, to the donations that made the war memorial a reality at the front of the School campus, being extended to the corner of Mountain Road and Clive Road to introduce boarding, the land exchange in 1963 that allowed the development of the motorway and the introduction of the lower fields and of course the purchase of corrections housing in 1994

that introduced the provision of teacher housing, followed most recently by the donation of the tennis courts in Clive Road - this all amounts to Grammar being an exceptional community asset.”

The focus now turns to securing 10 Clive Road. This key property will provide opportunities to anchor Tim’s vision for a growing Grammar precinct in the neighbourhood. “Grammar has enjoyed a strong presence in Mountain Road and Clive Road since 1916, and that presence will not diminish. Instead, through a Grammar precinct, it will become stronger.”
