Reflections bythe Headmaster

“Grammar’s school culture permeates the School daily, starting with the full School assembly. “ These few words were part of my first impressions of Auckland Grammar School, shared in my first Ad Augusta editorial in May 2013.

I raise this reflection, 13 years on, after contemplating not only what our young men achieved in Term 1, 2026, but also on a culture that has challenged them to succeed primarily in their studies, as well as in their extracurricular commitments and personal lives.

As you read this edition of Ad Augusta, you will not only see diversity of achievement, but you will read of the environment we aspire to create and maintain, without compromise.

My précis of Grammar in May 2026 is that it is a School with a clear educational philosophy and a defined culture that permeates it with deliberate intent and regularity, making it a high-purpose environment.

In The Culture Code, Daniel Coyle says, “successful groups communicate their purpose with the subtlety of a punch in the nose. They devote a surprising amount of time to reminding each other precisely what they stand for and repeating it, ad infinitum.”

Auckland Grammar School knows who it is. To some, our School is shrouded in mystery, while others think the successes Grammar boys have achieved over 157 years stem from privilege or a mystical drive from unique and talented young men. I would suggest otherwise.

It starts with assembly. It is the central point of our day, without exception. Our daily assembly reflects who we are. The ritual of ringing the handbell, the reading of the School Prayer, and the precision of our young men standing and being seated may be simple to some, but in a Grammar environment, these are beacons that focus everyone present. These beacons are signals that endorse our culture and reflect a high-purpose environment each day.

What follows is very clear messaging. Successes are to be celebrated. This is symbolised by ‘crossing of the stage’, which is earned and respected by the wider School and masters alike.

Successes celebrated in the year to date began with the naming of Nico Stanley as the 2026 Head Prefect, followed by the 2025 Scholars’ assembly with Taejeong Kim named the top scholar, through to the Premier Tennis team being named National Champions, and the Senior Eight Rowing crew earning Silver at the Maadi Cup in Twizel.

Our daily assembly is where our standards are clearly communicated and repeated. As Daniel Coyle says, “high-purpose environments are filled with small, vivid signals designed to create a link between the present moment and a future ideal. They provide two simple locators that every navigation process requires: Here is where we are, and Here is where we want to go.”

While abstract, the School’s educational philosophy supports where we are in 2026 and the style of education we will provide in the future. Specifically, it states, “the guardians of Auckland Grammar School have a responsibility to ensure that the minds of those in their care are trained with reason and vigour, while allowing their education to be strengthened by challenge and debate.”

At a time of considerable change in the New Zealand education system, our educational philosophy and the high-purpose nature of our environment should provide our community with reassurance. Change is not to be feared; it must first align with our philosophy and culture to be implemented at Auckland Grammar School.

The permeation of our culture is deliberate and planned. It supports a philosophy that is focused on the teaching of foundational content knowledge. However, a Grammar boy also knows, through assembly messaging and role modelling, that the standard he walks past is the standard he accepts in his life. This flows through to his understanding that learning can be difficult, but that if he embraces mistakes and challenges, he will learn from them rather than making excuses.

A Grammar boy knows he should be continually working to expand his belief in what he is capable of. But he has to accept the challenge himself, work in a self-disciplined fashion and use the environment he is part of to his advantage if he is to grow in anti-fragility and independence.

There is no mystical formula for individual or group success. It is about building a high-purpose environment where we share a steady stream of ultra-clear signals that align with our educational philosophy and our shared goal of having students regularly achieve personal bests.

Per Angusta Ad Augusta.

Tim O’ConnorHeadmaster
