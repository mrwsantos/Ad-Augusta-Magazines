The Sir John Buchanan Scholarship

Lady Rosemary Buchanan lives in London, but every year in April she comes to New Zealand for a morning tea at Tibbs House to meet “my newest Tibbs House boy.” Form 3 student Faifailemu Ah-Tuna has just started his Grammar journey as a Tibbs House boarder and is doing well. He is the 2025 recipient of the Sir John Buchanan Scholarship. This scholarship gives Faifailemu a full five-year boarding scholarship and it is named after Lady Buchanan’s late husband, Sir John, who passed away in 2015.

Sir John Buchanan ‘56 set up the scholarship in 2013 in recognition of the role Auckland Grammar School played in his life. He felt his attendance at Grammar set him up well and wanted to give other boys the opportunity to have a Grammar education. The scholarship is awarded to a Year 8 student who preferably lives in South Auckland where John grew up. Rosemary also lived in South Auckland and says that the area’s lack of proximity to Auckland Grammar School was part of Sir John’s desire to get the scholarship underway. “John lived in Papatoetoe and used to get the train into Newmarket every day to attend Grammar. He said to me that the School ‘gave me the best break I ever had’ and as such he wanted other out-of-zone boys to have the same opportunity.”

While Sir John was alive, he took an active role in looking for potential leaders and role models to take up the scholarship and was thrilled to meet his first two boys who took up the opportunity in 2014 and 2015. After his death, Lady Buchanan and Sir John’s brother Richard Buchanan ‘61 and wife Adele, have willingly continued Sir John’s keenness to meet the scholarship awardees, and follow their journey through Grammar and beyond.

Lady Buchanan was thrilled to see that two previous scholarship recipients also attended the morning tea. Faleseu Fepuleai ‘14 is a Training Intern at Auckland City Hospital and arrived in his medical attire while on call. He was pleased he could get there. “I love coming back to Grammar to see Lady Buchanan if I can, and luckily for me I work close by, so I could make it happen.” The two talked about his life post-school much to Lady Buchanan’s delight. Makea Upu ‘20, the 2024 Tibbs House Head Prefect, is carving out a successful acting career on both sides of the Tasman. He arrived with a guitar and sang a poignant rendition of Stevie Wonder’s Isn’t She Lovely, which was an emotive moment for all attendees.

If he were still here, Sir John Buchanan would no doubt have felt his scholarship was serving the exact purpose he intended it for.
