Michelle McNeill

The 2025 Sir John Graham Staff Scholarship was awarded to Rachel Lory. Rachel started teaching at Auckland Grammar School in 2008, straight out of training college, as one of the youngest teachers the School had employed. “It was incredibly beneficial as a young teacher to start my career in an environment where there were such clear structures and systems in place, so that I could focus on delivering rigorous content in the classroom.”

An English master and a mentor for in-school trainee teachers, Rachel has spent her entire teaching tenure at the School. She has been involved in numerous extracurricular activities, including Football, Form 4 camps and managing the School’s Mountain Biking and Skiing teams, and is currently involved with the Interact Group. “The Interact Group provides a framework for students who want to volunteer to give back to the wider community.”

The scholarship will mean Rachel can undertake professional study at the Center for the Developing Adolescent (CDA) at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA). This will allow her to deepen her understanding of contemporary neurodevelopmental science as it applies to adolescents and develop the practical skills necessary to apply the cutting-edge research being pioneered by the CDA in a teaching, mentoring, and coaching space. She will also have some down time with her husband Matt and her seven-year-old son Julian at a couple of their favourite ski fields: Aspen Snowmass and Breckenridge in Colorado.

After almost two decades standing in assembly each morning looking at the Honours Boards around the Great Hall, Rachel was humbled to be named as this year’s recipient. “It is an honour to be recognised in this way, especially given Sir John Graham’s incredible legacy - and it is pretty exciting to get my name on the Staff Scholars Honours Board!”

Simcock Family Teacher Scholarship

Nico Leiseder

Established in 2021, the Simcock Family Scholarship is for a teacher “who has demonstrated enthusiasm for discovery and learning, being curious across topics, and imparting an infectious energy for learning” - but History master Nico Leiseder was not expecting to be named as the 2025 recipient. “I was quite a bit shocked and completely taken by surprise, I didn’t really expect to receive anything. It took me a couple of seconds to fully realise what was happening when my name was called!”

With a Master of Arts in History and Comparative Germanic Linguistics from the University of Zurich, Nico started at the School in 2021 as one of three inaugural students of the Auckland Schools’ Partnership Project (ASPP), run through the University of Waikato. After the year-long programme, where the trainee teachers work in their chosen school and are supervised by mentor teachers, Nico received his Postgraduate Diploma of Teaching, becoming eligible for teacher registration.

Nico has thoroughly enjoyed teaching at Grammar for the past five years andhas become fully ensconced in the extracurricular life of the School, as Master inCharge of the Model United Nations Assembly group and Master in Charge of Athletics from 2022 until the end of 2025.

The course he will be undertaking at the University of Cambridge will allow him to step back into teaching Scholarship History upon his return to Grammar. “The School’s curriculum is mostly focused on modern history. Being able to go back to my roots - looking into medieval history - will be an invigorating opportunity, taking courses under lecturers whose work I have studied in the past. I am also looking forward to staying in accommodations in 15th century Queen’s College.”

A Swiss national, Nico is also thrilled to have the opportunity to travel back home to see friends and family. “Since I will already be in the vicinity of continental Europe, I am hoping to catch up with a few expat friends in England and family in Switzerland. As the added cherry on top, I will be travelling in the middle of the European summer, so hopefully I will be lucky enough to catch that one sunny day in the UK!”

the simcock family teacher scholarship

The Foundation Trust’s annual Staff Awards were established 26 years ago to provide a method for recognising and rewarding high performing members of staff with the express goal of aiding in their recruitment and retention.

In 2025, thanks to many generous supporters, there were 64 cash awards, ranging in value from $2,000 - $10,000, presented at the end of Term 4. This equated to 27% of staff receiving awards, a percentage that both the Foundation Trust and the School are thrilled to have.

However, with so many hardworking and high calibre staff missing out on awards each year, this number falls short of where the Foundation Trust and the School would ideally like to have available, to ensure that the majority of deserving staff can be recognised, as well as financially rewarded for their efforts.

With the goal of having 70 awards available this year, the Development Office would love to hear from you if you are interested in establishing a new award. These can target a specific area of the School that you are passionate about or be more generalised.

To discuss establishing a new award, please get in touch with Major Gifts Manager Daniel Reddish via email at d.reddish@ags.school.nz
