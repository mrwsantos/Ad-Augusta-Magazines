ROGER MEREDITH HARRIS ‘46

Roger passed away on 10 October 2025, aged 92.

Roger was Auckland’s oldest living first class cricketer. A gifted sportsman in his last two years at the School, he nly played in the 1st XI Cricket team and the 1st XV Rugby team. In his final year - as Deputy Head Prefect - he captained the 1st XI, was vice-captain of the 1st XV, and was a part of the Senior Athletics team.

Roger went on to represent Auckland at Rugby at an Under 21 level. Cricket thenbecame the longer-term focus with Roger playing Plunket Shield Cricket for Auckland from 1955 through to 1973. In 1959 he was selected as the opening batsman for the National side, playing two tests against England. Roger had the rare distinction of appearing on the main ground at Eden Park in three sports (Athletics, Cricket and Rugby) and often returned as a regular attendee to watcha good game.

Shaun Charles Godwin Johnson ‘58

Shaun passed away on 18 December 2025, aged 81.

Shaun was only the second blind student to attend the School. As a child he learnt piano at the New Zealand Institute for the Blind where he lived for the first few years of his life. This musical talent grew into a career, and he became the resident pianist at Wellington’s James Cook Hotel in Wellington. In 2015, he was recognised by the Wellington Jazz Club for his long-term contribution to music. When asked how he learnt his craft for a 2019 news article without being able to read music, he stated, “I learn by ear, that’s the only choice I have. It is a matter of hearing something and playing it”.

brett william Russell ‘70

Brett passed away on 14 January 2026, aged 69.

Brett was a proud Old Boy, with Masters saying that he was a “polite and respectable student with a commendable attitude to school.” Brett worked as an Accountant within the building and fastening industry. He is survived by his wife Victoria and his four adult sons, including Glenn, himself an Old Boy from the class of 2001.
