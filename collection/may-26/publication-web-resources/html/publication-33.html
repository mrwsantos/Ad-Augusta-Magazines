The Thomson Family

The 2026 Deputy Head Prefect comes from a long line of Grammar men.

When Paul Thomson ‘88 turns up to watch his son play in the 1st XV for Auckland Grammar School, he has his own memories of walking out onto the Number 1 field to the thunderous support of the Augusta Army. By the time Paul left Grammar, he had spent three years in the 1st XV (1991 to 1993). In his last year of school in 1993, he was also part of a New Zealand Secondary Schools side that dismantled the English and Australian touring teams with comfortable ease. The New Zealand team was a team of titans, featuring a young Jonah Lomu and nearly a dozen future All Blacks. It was a team that Paul’s close school friend and fellow Grammar 1st XV player, Jeremy Stanley ‘88, had played in the previous year.

Paul and Jeremy’s friendship, which started at school, continued well past schooling and rugby days. The friends have watched their families grow and now enjoy seeing their sons enjoy a friendship. Form 7 students Matayas (Taye) Thomson and Nico Stanley are also fellow 1st XV

players, just as their dads were over 30 years ago. Nico and Taye also lead the 2026 Prefect cohort, with Nico as Head Prefect and Taye as Deputy Head Prefect. Taye describes working with Nico as “surreal. We have known each other since we were very young, and our families are very close.” He says accepting the role of Deputy Prefect is an honour. “The School has a strong, long-lasting history and I believe being a part of it is very special. It has given me many opportunities and helped shape who I am today.”

Taye knew about Auckland Grammar School well before he stepped foot inside the gates. “My dad told me a lot about Grammar. He told me many chool stories, discussed the culture and history of Grammar and always took me to Grammar vs Kings 1st XV games. I also spent quite a lot of time with his schoolmates growing up, which really embedded Grammar in me from a young age.”

Taye also knew about the generations further back. While his grandfather was

not an Old Boy, his great grandfather Hugh Thomson ‘20 and Hugh’s brother Lewis Thomson ‘22 did attend the School.

Further back, Hugh and Lewis’ uncle, Frank Thomson, started at Grammar in 1875, just six years after its opening in 1869. W. Thomson has also been found in the school records as attending Grammar in 1871 for a few months. This is presumed (but cannot be confirmed) to be Frank’s older brother William. In those days the School’s campus was the Albert Barracks on the corner of Princes and O’Rorke Streets in the central city.

In 1878 the School moved to the District Court building in Eden Street. While some students were taught there, the lack of space meant others were at an old Māori Chapel in Parliament Street and St Andrew’s School in Symonds Street.

The ‘missing’ generation of Old Boys is Paul’s father, Ernest. Ernest’s family lived in Takapuna, so he went to Takapuna Grammar School. He was a talented student who was Dux of the school.

However, Ernest’s father Hugh Thomson ’20 was much closer to Auckland Grammar School. Hugh and his brother Lewis ’22, were about as close as you could get. The family home was 8 Clive Road, right beside Tibbs House. This house has since come into the School’s possession for staff housing.

Perhaps a portent for other future property purchases for the School, Hugh played tennis a few doors down at Ngatira Tennis Club which is now the Auckland Grammar School Tennis Centre.

The Thomson family history notes Hugh was a ball boy for an exhibition match there between two Australian Davis Cup Players - Jack Hawkins and Gerald Patterson - held under lights in 1925. Patterson was a world-class player (a two-time Wimbledon champion), so his appearance at Ngatira was the 1920s equivalent of having a Top 10 player today

show up for a local club match. It was, no doubt, a big honour for 18-year-old tennis fan Hugh to be the ball boy.

Sadly, Paul’s father Ernest and his grandfather Hugh both passed away before Paul started Auckland Grammar School - a school he very nearly didn’t attend. “I was destined for Dilworth. However, I pleaded with Mum to let me go to Grammar, and she changed her mind at the last minute.” If they had been alive, Paul’s father and grandfather would no doubt have loved seeing him excel at rugby at Grammar and beyond.

Paul began teacher training in 1994, but with rugby turning professional in 1995, contracts soon beckoned. After being selected for the New Zealand Under 21 side in 1995 Paul went on to play for the Auckland team from 1995 to 2001, captaining the side to a National Provincial Championship victory in 1999. In this period Paul also played for New Zealand A (1997 to 1999) and the Blues (1997 to 2001). In 1997 the Blues were the Super 12 Championship-winning team. Paul also played six games for the Māori All Blacks between 1996 and 2001.

Son Taye is following a similar sporting excellence tradition. Along with 1st XV

rugby, he is playing his third year in the Premier Basketball team, earning his colours last year, given he played in the New Zealand Under 17 Basketball team. His talent in Basketball has him travelling globally this year. Taye played for the team in China at the end of April, and he is in Turkey for the Under 17 World Cup in late June. He is also in the Auckland Under 18 Basketball team.

Managing high level sport and doing well in his academic life at School takes commitment, which is acknowledged by his father. “Taye understands what Grammar is and how to make the most of it. The pride he shows for the lion is very evident in all his activities.”

Whatever life offers Taye post-school, he has some thoughts on the Grammar chapter of his life before it ends. “I will reflect on the fond memories, the School’s structure and tradition, and the people I have met and befriended. The School has given me many opportunities through academics, sports and now leadership, allowing me to pursue goals I might not have previously thought possible. It has helped shape the person I am today and taught me valuable lessons and values that I will continue to uphold.”
