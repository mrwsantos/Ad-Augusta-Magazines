asb polyfest

The annual Polyfest competition took place in late Term 1, with the School’s Samoan, Tongan and Kapa Haka groups participating.

Led by longtime tutor Francis Burke, the Samoan group had several young, inspirational tutors alongside student leaders Bitner Lokeni and Lukah Pao who helped lead the group through this transitional stage. The new tutors are part of the International performance group Tatau, an all-male Samoan Cultural group.

With just 25 students in the group, the group performed superbly against schools with 50+ students. The experience proved invaluable in preparation for the 2027 competition.

The Tongan group, with 37 students in total, began practicing in late February, balancing academic studies and other extracurricular activities with a rigorous rehearsal schedule right up until competition day. ARTS AND CULTURE

annual national youth jazz competition

The Music department at Auckland Grammar School recently concluded Term 1 with two successful performances at the National Youth Jazz Festival in Tauranga. The School’s Big Band and the Jazz Combo showcased the technical growth and discipline fostered throughout the term’s rigorous schedule.

The Big Band earned a Silver Award for an energetic and cohesive performance that impressed both the audience and the judging panel. The adjudicators specifically praised the trumpet section’s power and precision throughout the set.

The programme commenced with El Abrazo, establishing a high technical standard from the outset. A highlight was the ensemble’s rendition of Michael Bublé’s Feelin’ Good, featuring a standout vocal performance by Blake Lewis. Throughout the set, Josh Benson was recognised for his technical skill and melodic flair during an impressive tenor saxophone solo.

The Big Band’s finale of MacArthur Park was played with immense energy. This concluding piece served as a vehicle for several featured soloists, including Henry Wong on alto saxophone, Hanrui Liu on trumpet, and leader of the Big Band Ryland Te Tamaki on trombone. Each student delivered his solo with confidence and stylistic maturity, contributing to the ensemble’s overall success.

Following the Big Band, the Jazz Combo performed a sophisticated four-number set - Caravan, There Will Never Be Another You, Nardis, and Desire. This smaller format effectively highlighted the group’s individual musicianship and their collective ability to maintain a steady groove. The adjudicators noted the confident saxophone leadership and the solid foundation provided by the rhythm section, which allowed for fluid improvisation.

Henry Wong acted as the main soloist for the Combo, demonstrating significant creative range across the various jazz standards. The group benefited from a highly disciplined rhythm section, including strong and reliable bass playing from Josh Benson and structured, articulate piano work from Andy Cheng.

This rhythmic core was further enhanced by Fernando Montesinos Garcia, who provided several impressive drum solos that anchored the performance. The set successfully demonstrated students’ understanding of the nuances of small-group jazz.

Congratulations are extended to all students for their dedication and for representing the School so impressively, and to Elliot Gray for his support and leadership of the groups. The results achieved at the National Youth Jazz Festival reflect the hard work of the boys and the music staff during Term 1.
