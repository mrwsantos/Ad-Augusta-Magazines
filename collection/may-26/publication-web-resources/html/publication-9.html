student leadership

To start off, I would like to extend my congratulations to the Prefect cohort for 2026. This is a huge achievement and itspeaks volumes about your character and your commitmentto the School over the last four years.

I know that I, alongside Deputy Head Prefect Matayas Thomson and Senior Prefects Rupert Glatzer and Xavier Clark, am extremely excited to work with you all and lead the School with pride. You have all earned this role, but what matters now is how you carry it - lead with humility, set the standard every day, and remember that your actions will always speak louder than the badge.

The start of a new year always brings a sense of expectation.New classes, new challenges, new opportunities. But more than that, it brings a question that every one of us needs to ask ourselves. What am I going to make of this year? I ask that youreally think about this question and think about what you can do differently this year to maximise your potential. What can you doto be a contributing Grammar man? What can you do to getthe most out of 2026?

At Grammar, we talk a lot about excellence, whether it be academic, sporting, or cultural. But excellence doesn’t happenby accident. It comes from setting clear goals, committing to them, and having the discipline to follow through when things get hard. I challenge you to set clear goals for this year - no matter how big or how small. Whether it’s achieving a certain standardin your exams, making a sports team or simply stepping out of your comfort zone to make new friends - having something to work towards gives direction and purpose.

If I have learnt anything over my four years at Grammar, it is that this school rewards hard work, like in most things in life you get out of it what you are prepared to put into it. There is no doubtthat at times it can be challenging, and the pressure is high.

I have been fortunate enough to be a member of the 1st XV Rugby team over the last two years - a team that teaches you that pressure is a privilege. During these times of high pressure, I have realised that it reveals two kinds of people - those who let it limit them, and those who use it to rise to the occasion.

What person will you be? Whether it is through exams, musical performances, matches, or expectations, pressure is part of life at Grammar. There is always a choice with how much effort and

hard work you are prepared to put in. This lesson applies whether you are wearing a rugby jersey, wielding a hockey stick, or simply wearing the Grammar uniform.

What defines us as a school is not how we perform when things are easy, but how we respond when they are hard. In Belonging, Owen Eastwood explains that strong groups perform best when individuals understand where they come from and the responsibility they carry for each other. Belonging is not passive - it is earned through contribution, standards, and shared values.

At Grammar, belonging means understanding that you are part of something far bigger than yourself: a tradition built by generations before us, and a culture that depends on how we act today. When we hold ourselves and each other to high standards, we create an environment where people feel supported, accountable, and able to perform.

To the Form 7 cohort, I trust that you will make the most of your last year at Grammar and be the example. Although it is our last year it is not the time to slow down but rather speed up and continue learning and growing. So, finish strong and make sure you have no regrets about your final year when you walk through the School gates for the final time.

So to finish, remember that excellence comes from effort, pressure is a chance to rise, not something to avoid, and belonging is earned through contribution, standards, and service. If we commit to these things, we strengthen not only ourselves, but the culture of this school. Stand tall, be proud, be united, and let your actions reflect what it means to be a Grammar man.

2026 Head Prefect Nico Stanley in his first address to the student body.
