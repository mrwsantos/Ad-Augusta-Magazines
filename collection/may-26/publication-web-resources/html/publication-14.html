The Auckland Seasonal Debating Tournament took place via Zoom in early March. The Grammar 1 team, consisting of senior students Benjamin Liu, Maskil Ma and Daniel Wang, had two wins against King’s College and Westlake Boys’ High School, with two losses to Rangitoto College and St Kentigern College.

The moots for the day covered the role of TikTok in mental health treatment, long-term disaster management in New Zealand, penalising New Zealanders moving to Australia and the most effective way to generate institutional change as a member of minority groups. All debates were limited preparation arguments.

The Grammar 2 team, consisting of senior students Toby O’Sullivan, YiChen Qin, Cheng Xi Wu and Liam Yeh, also had two wins and two losses, while the Grammar 3 team had a win and three losses on the day.

The teams wish to thank Charlotte Forster, Barbara McGowan, Sam Poole and Reece Wright for their support for the tournament.

Established in 1956 by HRH Prince Philip and German educator Kurt Hahn, the Duke of Edinburgh’s International Award is a prestigious and highly recognised award that is well regarded by universities and employers. The Award enables rangatahi (young people) to develop life skills that will enable them to build a better future for themselves and their communities.

There are three levels of the Award: Bronze, Silver and Gold. Foreach Award, participants need to complete four specific sections: development of a new skill, practice of a physical activity, participation in some form of community service, and completion of an adventurous journey.

Being picked up at Mercer Bay marked the end of a demanding but rewarding experience, and we aim to build upon it during our Qualifying Journey in May.”

Recognition in the world of Wearable arts

Form 5 student Arvin Rafiee was one of 695 entries in the TOI Awards at this year’s World of Wearable Art event. ‘Toi’ means ‘art’ in te reo Māori and the awards are entered by students under the name of their school in Junior or Senior categories. Arvin’s talent saw him announced as a national finalist in the Senior category.

Arvin entered in the ‘Stories of the South Pacific’ category which acknowledged Māori and Pasifika culture. His piece was an abstract, couture work with many influences and inspiration from Māori culture. The end result took many hours of his time. “My creation was a testament to the craftsmanship and detail of Māori culture, the time it takes to craft such pieces and the value and cultural significance it holds.”

His entry information about ‘Ngā Pūtake o Papatūānuku’ (‘The roots of Papatūānuku’) reads: “As we enter a new world, this garment reminds us to honour where we began. The symbol of a Koru reflects growth and new beginnings, while harekeke leaves represent the whenua; the lands that ground us. In embracing the future, we must not forget Papatūānuku and the taonga of te tatiao. This is a tribute to the tangata whenua, to whakapapa, and the beauty of what has always sustained us.”

Arvin entered because creating clothing has always been of interest to him since he was young. “Growing up, I’ve allowed myself to express art and meaning into clothing, making them more like pieces compared to something you’d frequently wear.”

Given his interest in the arts, Art as a subject is something he has always enjoyed in his schooling. “It’s a way to express anything you feel in fun and abstract ways.” Art teacher Justin Boroughs was the person Arvin went to when he first had the idea of entering TOI under Grammar’s name. “He supported my idea and when the competition was over, communicated with the School to have it displayed in Te Ara. The immediate support he showed is something I will always be very grateful for. “

Justin knows talent when he sees it, saying Arvin is not only gifted at drawing and painting, but is also hugely creative. “He has a well-developed imagination, and thinks in a unique way.” That creativity is something Arvin hopes might turn into a career in the future, with a goal of starting his own label one day.

relay for life fundraiser

In mid-March, 40 students from the Interact Group participated in the annual Relay for Life, raising funds for the Cancer Society.

With a 7am start and fuelled by breakfast, students walked the first lap together in silence, providing an opportunity to reflect on family and friends affected by cancer, and to remember those who had been lost. Many students demonstrated remarkable endurance, with several running the equivalent of a half-marathon.

Special thanks go to masters Marie Gaspar, Yasmin Lazarus, Neitana Lobb, Rachel Lory and Mellisa Pennington for their time and support throughout the day.

Members of both the Distance Squad and the Interact Group took part in the Relay for Life fundraiser, raising funds for the Cancer Society.
