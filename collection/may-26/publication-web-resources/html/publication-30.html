As a youngster, Old Boy Fehi Fineanganofo ‘16 started playing Rugby League; it wasn’t until he started his tenure at Auckland Grammar School that he transitioned to Rugby Union. Fehi was invited to attend the Barbarians Under 18 Development Camp in 2020 - his final year at the School - where he was named in the New Zealand Secondary Schools team. Sadly, the competition hadto be scrapped due to the COVID-19 pandemic.

At the 2024 Summer Olympic Games in 2024, Fehi scored two back-to-back tries for the All Blacks Sevens in their first game of competition against Japan. This came just a year after he made his debut for the side in Los Angeles in 2023, where he wasdescribed as “New Zealand’s breakthrough star of 2024”.

Upon his return to Aotearoa, Fehi signed a two-year contract with the Hurricanes ahead of the 2025 Super Rugby Pacific season. He is currently the 2026 Super Rugby Pacific’s leading try scorer with 15 tries in 10 games, having scored a Super Rugby record of three hat tricks this season. Fehi is now just one try short of the all-time season record with four regular matches left to play.

It is Fehi’s wish to see the Hurricanes win the second Super Rugby trophy; a wish that is somewhat bittersweet, as he has signed with English club the Newcastle Red Bulls, leaving the Hurricanes at the end of the current season. In his own words, the higher salaries

that can be obtained overseas were too hard to resist. “I just want to provide for my family and try another opportunity. But I can’t wait to start a new chapter with Newcastle and the brand of rugby they want to bring to the Premiership.”

Acting in the lead role of Tateh in the combined School production with Epsom Girls’ Grammar School of Ragtime: The Musical in 2018 likely seems a long time ago for George Ferrier ‘14. A lot can happen in eight years.

These days, he has a list of international productions to his name, but he remembers that time fondly. “It was a good way to round out my last year at Grammar, with a great team of people. Ragtime was a true ‘feel good’ production, whether you were involved in it on stage or in the crew, or coming along to watch it.”

By 2018, George had already secured a regular role as Leo in the New Zealand television series Dirty Laundry (2016) and knew this was a career path he wanted to advance. Formal training for George started when he was accepted into a competitive 2018 / 2019 Summer School Program at The Juilliard School in New York. He then later graduated from the prestigious Stella Adler Academy of Acting & Theatre in Los Angeles.

George is now a familiar face internationally, having had a regular role as TJ in the series One of Us Is Lying (2021 - 2022) and a starring role as Sam, alongside Academy Award nominee Charlotte Rampling in the film Juniper (2021). George reflects that the opportunity to be cast alongside Charlotte offered further training in his craft of a different kind. “Being on set with Charlotte was really a masterclass in acting every day.”

George went on to play Jordan in the Netflix series Sweet Tooth (2023 - 2024) before embarking on his latest project - the Netflix hit series The Hunting Wives (2025 - 2026).

The series sees George play Brad, a quintessential Texas high school star who is suspected of murdering his girlfriend while being entangled in a high-stakes, illicit affair with his mother’s best friend. The steamy thriller, set in Texas, has dominated streaming charts worldwide, cementing George’s status as one of New Zealand’s most successful young acting exports.

When Cyril Moore ‘46 decided to write his memoir, the name - A Full on Life - was completely appropriate for a man who has lived a busy and eclectic life. As he says, “There are many words you could use to describe my life, but boring is not one of them.”

Perhaps being an only child in the 1930s depression shaped him to be outward looking, with an acute awareness of people in need. These traits have seen him spend countless hours in service to his community, from the scouting movement to Lions International, and many other charitable causes in between.

After Grammar, Cyril wanted to go to medical school in Otago. However, his parents did not want to lose their only child to Dunedin, so he took their wishes into account and began studying Civil Engineering at the University of Auckland. The medical world still beckoned, and after one year of his degree he cross-credited his papers towards pharmacist training after finding an apprenticeship. By age 20, he was Chief Pharmacist at Middlemore Hospital. His memoir has fascinating stories of his time dispensing medicines from the hospitals of Auckland city to the small communities around Maraetai.

Cyril went on to enjoy another career as a highly successful travel agent, studying at night and qualifying in three years, rather than the usual seven, while still working as a pharmacist. He travelled extensively himself as the District Governor of Lions Pacific and with his late wife Jan, “the love of my life.”

A stuffed lion toy has pride of place in his office - a testament to his connection to Lions and the multiple awards he has received from them for his outstanding service. However, he is also staunchly loyal to another lion, being a Grammar Old Boy. Cyril’s memoir talks fondly of his school days and his unwillingness as a prefect to punish students with detention. “All my life I have been more interested in people’s loyalty and friendship rather than punishing them.” That’s Cyril - a man with the heart of a lion.

When Simon Denny, MNZM ‘96 reached out to his old Art teacher at Auckland Grammar School, Justin Boroughs was thrilled to hear from him. “I always love hearing from former students, and Simon is perhaps one of our most famous Old Boy artists now.” Justin reveals that Simon’s introduction to the Art department came later than most. “He gained all of his qualifications in Form 6 and decided in Form 7 to take five completely new subjects, with one of them being Painting.”

After studying at the Elam School of Fine Arts at the University of Auckland, Simon’s career took him overseas. He has exhibited globally - renowned for artworks that unpack stories about technology using painting, web-based media, installation, sculpture, print and video. Simon lives and works in Berlin and serves as a Professor of Time-Based Media in Hamburg.

The connection from Simon was more than just a catch up, it was to donate an artwork to the School. As curator of the Headmaster’s Art Collection, Justin was delighted to accept the piece. “The painting is a significant addition to the collection because it adds further context to a theme that runs through the collection, that of landscape painting. The painting takes into account our increasingly digital world and is, in effect, a modern interpretation of a landscape painting.”

Simon’s inspiration came from “the proliferation of virtual worlds called ‘metaverses’, and the highly speculative market where people bought digital tokens that represented plots of land in these metaverses.” The designs reminded him of modernist paintings, often based on square, gridded motifs, like those of Dutch painter Piet Mondrian. “I first learned about the connections that could be made between landscape painting and modernist abstraction in the painting department at Grammar. I thought a painting from this series I created - which builds directly on knowledge I learned at Grammar - would therefore be a great fit for the School.”
