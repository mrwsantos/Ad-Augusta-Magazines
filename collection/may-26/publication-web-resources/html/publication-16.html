The 58th annual National Concerto Competition was held in Christchurch inearly March. Established in 1967, It is New Zealand’s most distinguished classical competition for talented musicians under the age of 25 years, and provides an opportunity to perform, a place to celebrate talent, and aplatform to launch national and international careers.

Form 5 student Hayden Chiu and Form 4 student Ryan Yeh were chosen to perform their respective works in a public performance, accompanied by the Christchurch Symphony Orchestra. Hayden performed Violin Concerto No. 2 in D minor, Op. 22 by Polish violinst Henryk Wieniawski. Hayden was intrigued by Henryk’s style, stating that “he understands how the violin should be played”. Ryan’s piece was called Variations on a Rococo Theme for Cello by Russian composer Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky

Both students achieved remarkable success, with Hayden placing secondand Ryan gaining third place, behind 21-year-old overall winner Lorna Zhang.

The 2026 Major School production, hosted by Epsom Girls’ Grammar School and featuring students from Auckland Grammar School, will be Les Misérables, a musical by Alain Boublil and Claude-Michel Schönberg.

The musical, based on Victor Hugo’s novel of the same name, followsex-convict Jean Valjean’s lifelong quest for redemption in early 19th-century France. After breaking parole to start a new life, Valjean is relentlessly pursued by Inspector Javert while caring for Cosette, the daughter of the tragic Fantine. Set against the backdrop of the 1832 Paris student revolution, the story explores profound themes of justice, love, and sacrifice.

With the auditions for the lead roles completed early in Term 1, six Auckland Grammar School students will play the male leads - Blake Lewis (Jean Valjean), Sam Beggs (Javert), Josh Rowbotham (Thenardier), Ethan Payne (Gavroche), James Fearon (Marius) and Jacob Bethell (Enjolras) - and nine musicians.

At the time of printing, the show was halfway through its seven-show run held at the Raye Freedman Arts Centre, with the closing night on Saturday 16 May.

international DEPARTMENT

David Shin from SiS International Education has been bringing secondary school students from Korea to New Zealand for over 30 years. With offices in Korea, Auckland, Tauranga, Wellington and Christchurch he is well placed to inform families in Korea about the New Zealand education system.

He particularly enjoys telling prospective students and their parents about Auckland Grammar School and why he thinks the School might be a good fit for them. His conversation is plainly memorable, because every year a new group of students from Korea walk through the arches of Mountain Road, ready to start their Grammar journey.

The School’s International Relationship Manager, Lee Lim, notes that David brings the most international students to Grammar year on year. “Since 2020 alone, he has successfully placed close to 30 students here at the school, demonstrating his continued trust in us and his strong commitment to matching students with the right academic environment.” David’s recommendation of Auckland Grammar School is deeply personal. Having graduated from one of Korea’s top academic high schools himself, he reflects on his first visit to the School 30 years ago. “When I walked into the Great Hall, I felt an instant connection to my own school days. I recognised the same rigorous academic standards, strong intellectual culture, and long-standing tradition of excellence. They are qualities that convinced me back then that Auckland Grammar is truly one of the best schools in New Zealand, and I have seen this to be true over the course of the past three decades.”

His belief is further reinforced by the success of students who come through the School. Kim Taejeong, a student placed by David was named the 2025 Dux. Another is Kim Byeongsub, also a David-referred student, who graduated from Grammar in 2016. Kim went on to graduate from The University of Auckland and is now working as a medical doctor in Hamilton. These success stories reflect David’s careful guidance and his ability to identify students who are well suited to Auckland Grammar’s academic culture.

A great academic reputation is a key, but there is far more to a good education, David says. “This is most definitely a place where students can maximise their academic ability, but what I also like is how this environment values well-rounded men too. Grammar supports our students to grow into confident and capable young men. The sporting and extra-curricular opportunities on offer here are extensive, which is different to school in Korea.”

The relationship between Auckland Grammar School and David’s company SiS is a close and collaborative one. Each year, Rachel Fenton - Director of International at the School - participates in the Education Fairs that David organises in Korea to meet prospective parents and students. When students arrive in New Zealand, David and Rachel’s teams collaborate on pastoral care. Regular communication between parents, students, homestays, school and SiS, with the shared goal of growing a great Grammar man, is vital, and on-going. The vast number of SiS students through Auckland Grammar School over the past 30 years would no doubt attest to that care, from recruitment to graduation.
