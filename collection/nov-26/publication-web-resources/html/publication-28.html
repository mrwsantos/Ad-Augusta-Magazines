all black #56 for the School

26-year-old Saula Ma’u ‘14 became All Black #1242 in August 2026, and the 56th All Black for Auckland Grammar School.

Called up to the All Blacks squad as an injury replacement during their tour of South Africa in August, Saula was named on the bench for a midweek match against the Lions. His official start in the black jersey came when he was called up to play the final 20 minutes of the match, with the result being in the All Blacks’ favour with a 41-35 win.

Saula’s first post-match interview was emotional. “I am just grateful for the coaching staff, for the All Blacks giving me a chance to represent my family, my friends and my village back home. Hopefully I made them proud.” It was aptly conducted by former All Black Jeff Wilson. The men share a connection as Harbour Rugby Football Club alumni. And like Jeff before him, Saula currently plays for the Highlanders.

The 140kg, 193cm Tongan-born front-rower was a graduate of the John Jones Steel High-Performance Programme and debuted for the Highlanders in round 3 of the 2022 Super Rugby Pacific Competition. Prior to 2022, a run of injuries had limited his game time, but he maintained his focus on carving out a professional rugby career. In 2025 and 2026, he became a key figure in the Highlanders’ front row, making his 50th appearance for the team in May 2025.

His 2026 rugby season changed with the phone call he had always hoped might happen one day. However, the reality of the ask to pack a bag and head to the other side of the world, caught him off guard.

“A couple of weeks ago I was back at home playing for Otago and then two weeks later I’m playing for the All Blacks - it just feels amazing.”

Saula, like 55 men before him, is living proof that the All Blacks dreams of a 1st XV player at Grammar (for Saula, 2016 to 2018) can indeed come true.

the school’s 55th all black

23-year-old Fehi Fineanganofo ‘16 was named as All Black #1234 by coach Dave Rennie in June 2026. He also has another number to his name that he is proud of: All Black #55 for Auckland Grammar School. The School has the record of producing more All Blacks than any other secondary school in New Zealand.

Fehi started off playing Rugby League in Onehunga, before switching to Rugby when he started at Auckland Grammar School. Grammar’s All Black Rugby heritage, he says, was something he quickly picked up on, and quietly stashed away as a possible goal one day. When he did make the All Blacks, it made for some poignant reflection of a ten-year journey since entering the Mountain Road gates in 2016. “Being a Grammar boy, I saw all the All Blacks boys that used to go to Grammar. It’s kind of just been a dream, ever since going there.”

His talent at the game saw him make the 1st XV at Grammar in Forms 5, 6 and 7 years (2018 to 2020). In 2023 he started playing in the National Provincial Championship (NPC), with the Bay of Plenty Steamers where they made the finals that year. After turning his hand to the Sevens, competing in the 2024 Paris Olympics, he signed a two-year contract with the Hurricanes, making his official debut in Super Pacific Rugby in 2025. During his second season in 2026, Fehi scored

a record-breaking 17 tries, one in the final to help the Hurricanes defeat the Chiefs 60-5 and claim the 2026 title.

Fehi started 2026 with a plan to move to the English Premiership club Newcastle Red Bulls later in the year, but his call up for the All Blacks has seen a welcome change in direction and a schoolboy dream come true.

From the classrooms of Auckland Grammar School to the podium in Glasgow, Daniel Morton has developed into one of New Zealand’s most promising young cyclists, earning selection for the national team while still in his teens and steadily progressing through the international ranks.

While a student at Auckland Grammar School, Daniel ‘19balanced the demands of academic life with the increasingly competitive cycling programme. His commitment to training and racing saw him emerge asone of the country’s leading junior endurance riders.

In 2024, Daniel enjoyed a breakthrough season, winning three Oceania junior titles and representing New Zealand at the UCI Junior Track World Championships in China.

Daniel’s transition to senior competition was equally impressive. In 2025, he claimed the New Zealand Madison Championship alongside Magnus Jamieson and continued to establish himself as both a road and track cyclist. Success on the road, including a stage victory at the Tour of Southland, demonstrated his versatility, while strong performances in Nations Cup events confirmed his place in New Zealand’s endurance squad.

The highlight of Daniel’s career so far came at the 2026 Commonwealth Games in Glasgow. Racing as part of the Men’s Team Pursuit quartet, he helped the team ride to a Bronze medal, defeating Wales for third place. It was Daniel’s first Commonwealth Games and a fitting reward for years of dedication, resilience and relentless pursuit of excellence. “It felt like a bit of a fever dream standing on the podium and seeing my family in the crowd. I couldn’t stop thinking that it had all started when I signed up for Cycling at Grammar in Form 3!”

Daniel’s journey from Auckland Grammar School to the podium atthe Commonwealth Games reflects the values of perseverance, humility and ambition. His achievements have made him a proud representative of New Zealand, demonstrating that sustained commitment and hard work can carry talented young athletes from School sport to the international stage.

The Men’s Team Pursuit quartet. From left: Tom Sexton, Daniel Morton ‘19, George Jackson and Marshall Erwood.

Auckland Grammar School is proud to celebrate the appointment of Old Boy Willie Rickards ‘98 as Head Coach of the All Blacks Sevens, a role he will hold through to the 2028 Olympics in Los Angeles. Willie’s Rugby journey began on the School’s No. 1 field, where he played centre for the 1st XV, before going on to represent Southland, Taranaki, the Highlanders, and the All Blacks Sevens. He also gave back to his alma mater, working as a part-time Rugby administrator before moving into coaching full time.

Willie’s elevation to the top job follows two seasons as the assistant coach for the All Blacks Sevens, capping a coaching career spanning more than 14 years across club, provincial union, national and international rugby. He succeeds Tomasi Cama, who stepped down after the 2026 World SVNS Series.

Willie’s coaching journey has included a National Championship with the Taranaki Sevens programme, an NPC Premiership win as Taranaki’s Skills Coach, and a Ranfurly Shield triumph over Canterbury as Taranaki’s Head Coach. Two Rugby heavyweights will also join the new-look coaching group alongside Willie, with former All Blacks Sevens captain Scott Curry stepping in as Assistant Coach, and former All Black Liam Messam taking on the role of Performance Coach.

The 27th HSBC SVNS Series returns in November 2026, visiting Dubai, Cape Town, Singapore, Perth, Vancouver and New York, before the World Championship in Hong Kong, Valladolid and Bordeaux, with every campaign now a step towards the 2028 Olympics.

Reflecting on the appointment, Willie was extremely humble. “To be entrusted with leading the All Blacks Sevens is an incredible privilege. This team has played a significant role in my life, first as a player and now as a coach, so to be given the opportunity to lead the programme is both humbling and exciting.”
