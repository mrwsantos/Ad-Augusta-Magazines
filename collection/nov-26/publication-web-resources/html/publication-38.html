terence graham (terry) nuthall ‘53

Terry passed away on 10 August 2026, aged 86.

Terry was a sound academic during his four years at Auckland Grammar School. He was in the A stream classes for Forms 3 and 4 and won first in class for 5E in 1955. Masters said of him that he was a “very keen learner, who has worked well and was a pleasure to teach.”

In addition to his career as a banker, Terry was an avid Hockey player, playing for all four years across various age grades. Terry also loved Orienteering, and represented New Zealand in the sport from 1989-2001. He was a lifelong member of the Auckland Orienteering Club and despite poor health, he was still helping at events up until his untimely passing.

An excerpt from an article he wrote for a magazine 40 years ago showed Terry’s more humourous side, poking fun at himself for picking up the wrong map at the start of a course. “I won’t take the wrong map again in a hurry but then there are a dozen other things that can go wrong. The answer is to concentrate.”

Terry was a regular at the annual Vintage Luncheon, as well as a grandfather to eight grandchildren and a great-grandfather to two.

peter brian riley ‘56

Peter passed away on 1 July 2026, aged 84.

Peter started his secondary schooling at Mahurangi College in Warkworth, before transferring to Auckland Grammar School for his final two years, ending up in 6A in 1959 and the Upper Sixth in 1960. Peter was an avid Rugby player and was also the recipient of the Williamson Scholarship.

After graduating from Grammar, Peter received his Bachelor of Engineering from the University of Auckland, before obtaining a Master of Science, majoring in Rock Mechanics from the University of London.

Peter was the founder and managing director of Riley Consultants Ltd. Established in 1984, the company is a multi-disciplinary engineering firm operating around the country and the Pacific, and was “driven by a desire to do things differently and to build something of Peter’s own”. Managing Director Steven Price said of Peter’s passing, “We will take time to reflect on Peter’s legacy and the lasting impact he has had on our organisation.”

dr michael john knapton ‘55

Michael passed away on 11 August 2026, aged 85.

Michael was born in Bournemouth, England, in 1941. During his time at Auckland Grammar School, he was a strong all-round student and athlete. In1955, he placed first in 3A and then progressed through 4A, 5A and 6A, playing Cricket and Rugby. Michael rose through the ranks of the School’s Cadet Battalion, serving as Sergeant and later Warrant Officer Class 2 in his final year.

Michael went on to study medicine at the University of Otago, graduating with a Bachelor of Medicine and Bachelor of Surgery. He served as Regimental Medical Officer for 6 RNZIR (Hauraki) from 1969 to 1985, reaching the rank of Major before retiring from the Army.

Following his retirement, Michael continued to serve the community as Honorary Physician to the Bay of Plenty Rugby Union from 1976 to 1996. An accomplished sailor, he also represented New Zealand in Half Ton yachting in 1972.

jon morris moses ‘66

Jon passed away on 13 July 2026,aged 74.

Jon attended Auckland Grammar School from 1966 to 1970, with masters saying that he “works very well, and was a very polite and helpful young man.”

Jon was a senior lecturer at Manukau Institute of Technology, a position he held for almost 37 years. He is survived by his wife Maryrose, his son Jon-Paul, his brother Malcolm ‘62 and his sister Sharon.

David John Fraser ‘46

David passed away on 27 August 2026, aged 94.

Despite missing almost a month of his first year at Auckland Grammar School due to mumps, David was in the A and B classes throughout his four years. David was named the Form 5 Form Rep, a keen athlete and was involved with Football, playing Junior Football in Form 3 and was named captain of the 1st XI side in his final year in 1949.

After leaving Grammar, David became a Justice of the Peace and was a Pharmacist for many years in the suburbs of Mount Roskill and Henderson. He was a life member of the Dunholme Tennis Club and was presented with a Gold Award for 20 years’ service in 1997.

David was part of a multi-generational family with sons Mark ‘69, Scott ‘70, Brett ‘73 and Gregory ‘74 attending the School along with his brothers Jim ‘49, Grant ‘50 and Craig ‘57. They all followed in their uncle John McLean Fraser’s footsteps who attended the School in 1921.
