INTERNATIONAL DEPARTMENT

Stephen and Jing Ecclestone reflect on the enjoyment of being a Homestay family for one of Auckland Grammar School’s international students.

Our international student, Ko Moriya, from Japan, is in the same year at school as our son Daniel (Form 6). Ko arrived at the beginning of Term 1, and we had initially agreed to host him for two terms. However, within just a few weeks of his arrival, we decided to extend this and host him for the full year.

If we had any trepidation about hosting a 16-year-old boy from another country, it completely disappeared once we met him and he settled into our family. We are sure it must also have been a nerve-wracking time for Ko, arriving in New Zealand and adjusting to life in a culture quite different from his own. Perhaps because we are both first-generation New Zealanders, it helps us understand some of the feelings a young person might experience when making that adjustment.

Ko has been a mature and engaged student, keen to learn about life in New Zealand, improve his English and generally make the most of his study-abroad experience.

The first term was largely about settling into life at Grammar and becoming familiar with the routines of school. We took Ko around Auckland and showed him some of the local sights, both to give him a broader picture of the city and to introduce him to life here from a more social perspective. We also had the opportunity to take him north to Waipu Cove, visit the

glowworm caves and spend a weekend in Rotorua. As a family his arrival was a great reason for us to engage in some iconic New Zealand experiences. These included visiting Te Puia to experience its geothermal attractions and Māori culture, walking among the trees at the Redwoods Tree Walk, and tackling the Canopy Tours ziplining experience.

By the second term, we had settled into the normal rhythm of daily life: school, homework, family time and social activities. Ko had quickly made friends with a number of his fellow students, helped in part by his involvement in social basketball.

Ko has enjoyed his time at Auckland Grammar School so much that he is considering staying for a second year. This would allow him to complete his secondary schooling in New Zealand and work towards gaining his university entrance qualifications.

Hosting Ko has been an extremely positive experience for our family. What began as an agreement to welcome an international student into our home for two terms quickly became a full-year commitment and an opportunity to share our family life, our city and some memorable New Zealand experiences with him. In return, Ko has brought his own culture, personality and perspective into our home, making the experience rewarding for all of us.

To enquire about becoming a Homestay parent please contact International Student Welfare Manager Sandra Heslin Boroughs via email at s.boroughs@ags.school.nz

INTERNATIONAL DEPARTMENT

International Student Welfare Manager Sandra Heslin Boroughs reflects on the Grammar Journey of new All Black Saula Ma’u ‘14. She and husband Justin Boroughs have come to refer to him as ‘their Tongan son.’

When Saula arrived from Tonga as a Form 4 student, no one could have known just how remarkable his journey would become. Quiet, humble, and gentle by nature, he arrived with a strong work ethic, a natural talent for rugby and a determination to make the most of the Auckland Grammar opportunity.

His English was limited, and communicating confidently in the classroom was initially a challenge. Rather than allowing this to hold him back, he quietly set about building his confidence, one step at a time. Saula’s progress was never about seeking attention or recognition. He developed in his own modest and unassuming way, steadily finding his voice in the classroom, building confidence with his peers, and becoming an increasingly influential presence both academically and onthe rugby field.

Saula excelled at sport. His talent, commitment and work ethic saw him develop into an outstanding rugby player. He worked hard to continually improve his skills and fitness, setting himself high standards and pushing himself to achieve his very best.

But perhaps what makes him most special is the way he has used his own experiences to support others. As a senior Pasifika student, Saula became a mentor and role model for younger Pasifika boys. He understood what it was like to arrive at Grammar, find your feet in a new environment and face the

challenge of building confidence in a different language and culture. He quietly encouraged others, offered support and led by example. He has always been a gentle giant - a young man whose presence commands respect, not because he demands it, but because of the person he has become.

Saula has embodied the seven Grammar values throughout his time at the School. These values have not simply been words for him; he has lived them every day through his actions, his attitude, his respect for others, his commitment to his studies and his dedication and passion for his chosen sport. His journey is a wonderful example of what can be achieved through determination, humility, hard work, and perseverance.Academically, he has continued to challenge himself and achieve at a very high level. On the rugby field, he has worked tirelessly to reach his potential. As a mentor, he has given back to those coming behind him. And as a young man, he has conducted himself with a quiet dignity that has earned him the respect and admiration of staff and students alike.

We are immensely proud of the accomplished young man he is today. His journey has been one of finding his voice, steady growth, resilience, and excellence. He has never needed to be the loudest in the room. Instead, he has let his actions speak for him. He leaves a wonderful legacy as a scholar, an outstanding sportsman, a mentor, a role model and, most importantly, a young man of great character. We know that wherever his journey takes him, he will continue to strive for excellence, support others, and remain the humble, respectful, and gentle giant we have been so privileged to know.
