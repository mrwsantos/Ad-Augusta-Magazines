The Director of Boarding changes at the end of the School year. After six years at the helm of Tibbs House, Frank Haszard ‘93 and his family are moving out and current Deputy Director of Boarding Matt Cuming and his family are moving in. Frank will continue to teach Economics, but with a longer commute.

The current Form 7 boarders are the cohort that Frank selected for their Form 3 start at Tibbs in 2022, so his timing to move on coincides with the end of their schooling. “I remember each and every interview with them back then. To see their growth and maturity and compare this to the sometimes nervous 12-year-olds they were, arriving for the first time, demonstrates the value of their Tibbs experience and the quality and strength of their character.” When asked about what he has loved about the job, he has a long list. “The goodwill of our community. Willingness to support the School and each other. Seeing the successes of our boarders across such a range of different fields. Seeing boys take advantage of the opportunities on offer with a Grammar education and seeing that potential being reached.” He will also miss the team. “Working with such a talented and committed group of quality staff has been fantastic.”

The boarding environment is also familiar to Matt, and he too appreciates seeing the journey first-hand from boy to young adult. “The most rewarding aspect has been guiding young men

through critical stages of their growth - watching them build self-reliance, character, and lifelong friendships. In a boarding environment, you play a pivotal role through the turbulent teenage years.”

Next year Frank and his wife Ella have a daughter starting intermediate and a son starting at Grammar. “They were born here in my first stint as a boarding master at Tibbs from 2011-2016 and grew up here as Tibbs kids, with all the benefits of that: security, regular interaction and making friends with other families, and the biggest and best backyard in Auckland across the road. Tibbs has been like an extended family for us all.”

Matt and his wife Rose have three children under the age of seven and also enjoy being part of the vibrant community. Matt feels having families living on site is also good for the boarders. “Living on site creates a natural, warm household dynamic rather than an institutional one. Having a young family around brings an authentic sense of home to the boarding house, which helps the boys, especially those far from their own families, feel grounded and comfortable.”

Matt is looking forward to his new role in 2027. “I am excited to continue refining our boarding environment, strengthen our house culture, and find new ways to support our boarders as they navigate various challenges.”

Reading Aloud

Staff Harmony

When Director of Library Catherine Ross approached members of staff with an idea to help grow engagement across the student body and to model and share reading, the response was immediate. The Reading Aloud series saw several staff, including Head of Latin Frances Hay-Mackenzie, English master Henry Lello and Head of English Charles Laing, read from a well-known novel to students in the presentation forum of Te Ara Mātauranga.

The idea came about after a session when well-known Old Boys would come in to read to students, so Catherine was excited to engage with the staff and students of the School. “It is really important for the students to see adults modelling reading, as we have seen that many of them don’t have adults that read, or even have any books, at home.”

Treasure Island by Robert Louis Stevenson was the book chosen for the series, a favourite of Henry’s. “Treasure Island is a complex literary text, but it is also a swashbuckling adventure. I think it is both fun and edifying for students and adults.” The book centres around Jim Hawkins, who finds a treasure map among the belongings of a dead pirate lodger. He sets sail aboard the ship Hispaniola to find the buried gold, unaware that the one-legged Long John Silver has secretly recruited much of the crew from his old pirate gang. When mutiny breaks out, Jim’s bravery helps the loyal men outwit the pirates, recover the treasure, and sail home.

Catherine sees a number of benefits in adults reading aloud to students. “There is considerable evidence that reading aloud to children enables them to process challenging content, text features and vocabulary, even in subjects not normally associated with reading aloud, such as science and technology.”

The Library staff are looking forward to the Reading Aloud series becoming a regular fixture throughout the year.

‘Period Zero’ vocalists Lauren Jeffares and Anton Elliot ‘11.

Each year, Headmaster Tim O’Connor issues a challenge to the School’s Staff Band - to perform on stage in assembly at leastonce before the end of the academic year. This year’s performance took place in the penultimate week of Term 2, continuing a tradition that spans more than three decades.

With founding member Doug Newton on lead guitar accepting the challenge, the group took to the stage with Head of Performing Arts James Ioelu on piano and vocals, Nico Leiseder on drums, English master Henry Lello on bass, and John Wilson on rhythm guitar. The band also welcomed two new vocalists in Chemistry master Lauren Jeffares and Old Boy Anton Elliot ‘11, the School’s Property Manager. Anton was invited by James to have a jam session with the band, but the penny dropped pretty quickly. “James didn’t specify where or when the jam would take place - he failed to mention it was on the Main Block stage during assembly.”

The band, now going under the moniker Period Zero, played three songs - Rocking in the Free World by Neil Young, Rock n’ Roll by Led Zeppelin as well as Anton’s personal favourite, Metallica’s Enter Sandman. “It provided the best entertainment to the students.”

Anton is no stranger to the stage, growing up in a house of performers, as both his parents were singers and theatre actors. “My brother Keegan ‘12 and I used to go with them and sit backstage while they would rehearse. During our time at Grammar, we did several of the School musicals (including Sweeney Todd in 2014), and we both remain active performers today.”

Anton received a number of overwhelmingly positive comments from both staff and students for his performance, his first time standing on the Great Hall stage. “I really enjoyed it though. It is nice to give the students a bit of a change from the normal assembly routine and I think this feeds a good culture.”
