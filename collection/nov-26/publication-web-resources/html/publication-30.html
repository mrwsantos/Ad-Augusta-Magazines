The maccormick brothers

Alastair ‘57 and Murray MacCormick ‘59 took a pilgrimage to Europe mid-year to trace their father’s steps in World War I. The trip covered a lot of ground - Gallipoli, the Somme and Le Quesnoy. But it was Passchendaele that took on special meaning for the brothers, as Murray reflects. “This is the place where we read about our father’s contribution in 1917, but he never spoke of it himself. Passchendaele was a bloodbath for New Zealanders making many returnees reluctant to speak of it. “So many young men struggled in dreadful conditions to gain a small advantage in a seemingly pointless conflict far from home.”

Their father Kenneth MacCormick (who started at Grammar in 1905) was a doctor, tasked with attending to the wounded. His work saw him Mentioned in Despatches (MiD) twice and made a Companion of the Distinguished Service Order (DSO). The citation notes his gallantry and devotion to duty. “He remained at duty for forty-eight hours without rest, visiting the advanced posts, searching shell holes, and bringing in many wounded. When one of his bearer posts was heavily shelled, with great coolness he got all the wounded away, staying behind himself until the last had left.” At Menin Gate in Ypres, Alastair and Murray laid a wreath in memory of their father’s service at Passchendaele in the 3rd Battle of Ypres.

The brothers visited the many sites where Commonwealth Soldiers are buried. Murray was moved by their sombre beauty. “Visiting the many moving war grave memorials and cemeteries totally belies the horrors that gave rise to them. Nowadays these beautifully-maintained graves are peaceful havens visited by thousands of their descendants, like us.”

Kenneth served in World War II as Director of Medical Services to the New Zealand Expeditionary Force in the Middle East. More information can be read about Kenneth and the five-generation MacCormick family on pages 70-73 of this edition of Ad Augusta.

oli welch ‘17

Ben Taylor and Old Boy Oli Welch ‘17 are members of the Avon Rowing Club and have absolutely taken the Rowing world by storm, becoming back-to-back world champions in the Men’s Coxless Pair for the second year in a row.

The pair have only been rowing together for a year, but their partnership was electric from the first outing. They won the Varese World Cup in June 2025 in their debut regatta, beating Olympic champions Valent and Martin Sinković from Croatia, before finishing second at Lucerne a fortnight later. Their season culminated with a Gold medal at the 2025 World Rowing Championships in Shanghai, where they won by five seconds in the final, New Zealand’s first world title in the event in years.

A year on, Ben and Oli have proved it was no fluke. The duo successfully defended their title in Amsterdam in August, dominating from start to finish to win in 6 minutes 16.62 seconds, nearly two seconds clear of the Men’s Pair from Australia. The victory took New Zealand’s total wins in the Men’s Pair to nine, the most of any nation in the event’s history.

Oli was full of relief and pride after the race, describing the feeling of crossing the line first for a second consecutive year. “We are just stoked to get it done. Another world title - it ispretty awesome. All this hard work hass paid off for the year so it is really satisfying.” It capped off a long season of training and racing for Ben and Oli, who had already claimed Gold at both World Cup regattas earlier in the year.

While still in the early stages of their partnership, Ben and Oli were also part of the World Team, a composite squad of standout athletes from non-qualifying nations, that competed at the inaugural World Rowing Shanghai Sprints, a new head-to-head 500-metre sprint format across six boat classes, which took place at the end of September.
