President’s Report

In June, I was honoured to be elected President of the Old Boys’ Association. It is a privilege to continue the legacy built by generations of Old Boys who have given their time over many decades.

I would also like to acknowledge outgoing President Murray McKinnon ’59, whose leadership over the past four and a half years has been outstanding. Murray’s contribution will be formally recognised at the Old Boys’ Dinner in early November. Since its creation over 130 years ago, the strength of the Old Boys’ Association lies in the generations of Old Boys continuing to give back, gather together and carry the story forward.

I had the pleasure of notifying three Old Boys that they were the 2026 Augusta Award recipients: Professor Richard Douglas ’75, Jonathan Moses ’75 and John Sparling ’51. They will be formally honoured at the Old Boys’ Dinner. Jonathan Ridler ’03 is the 2026 Lion Award winner and he will also be recognised at the event. All four are featured on pages 52 and 53 of this edition of Ad Augusta.

We note with sadness the recent passing of Stewart Matthews ’48. Stewart was a Prefect, Captain of the 1st XI Cricket team, a member of the Senior Athletics team and the founder of the Augusta Fellowship. We also note the passing of prominent Old Boys Peter Riley ’56 and Paul Cavanagh, KC ‘52

It has been a busy period for Old Boys events. In July, the Vintage Lunch welcomed 45 Old Boys and carried a special sense of occasion. It was from that event that a tradition, which began over 10 years ago, was re-established. Old Boys who reach 100 years of age will be presented with a special 100 Not Out 1st XV cap. The Headmaster had the honour of presenting these caps to Jim Espie ‘38 and Thomas Doo ‘39 at the annual Augusta Fellowship Luncheon in September. Laurie Hampton ‘39, Cliff Hart ‘40, John Kernohan ‘39 and Bruce Leighton ‘39 were sadly unable to make the luncheon, but their caps have been or will be delivered to their respective homes. Needless to say, when told, all six recipients were thrilled to have received such a thoughtful gift from the School.

Lastly, I would like to thank all current members of the Old Boys’ Executive Committee. Their involvement, together with the service of so many others, ensures the Association remains not only a guardian of tradition, but a living community; one that honours the School’s past, celebrates achievements, and continues to bring Old Boys together wherever they may be.

Per Angusta Ad Augusta.

Chris Sixton ‘86President of the Auckland Grammar School Old Boys’ Association
