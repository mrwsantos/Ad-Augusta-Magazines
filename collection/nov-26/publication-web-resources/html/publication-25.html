Peter Thompson ‘76 was appointed as a Companion of the New Zealand Order of Merit for services to philanthropy and rugby.

Peter has been Managing Director of Barfoot and Thompson since 2005, continuing a proud family legacy that stretches back more than a century. Beginning his career with the company in 1981, he progressed through a range of roles before taking on the leadership of one of New Zealand’s most successful real estate organisations. Barfoot & Thompson has developed a long-standing partnership with the Starship Foundation, raising more than $6 million since 2003 to support New Zealand children.

More broadly, Barfoot and Thompson have sponsored Mercy Hospice, major Auckland and Northland sports teams, community organisations and charities and events such as Ironman, Auckland Writers Festival, Auckland Marathon and Diwali celebrations. Peter also drove Barfoot and Thompson’s sponsorship of the World Masters Games in 2017 and has been involved as a sponsor with the New Zealand Olympic Committee since 2010.

Peter has been a Director of the Auckland Business Chamber since 2019. He has been a member of the Barbarians Rugby Club since 2008 and a key sponsor. He was Chairman of College Rifles Rugby Club from 1993 to 2007, helping instigate the first artificial turf at the Club.

Giving back to his community, Peter has coached or managed numerous rugby teams from school age with College Rifles to provincial sides for Auckland Rugby Union (ARU), also sitting on the ARU Board from 2008 to 2016.

Peter was thrilled to receive the award. “It is a great honour. It is not something one aims to get but a privilege to receive when offered - not just for me but also my family, particularly my wife Fiona who has attended many charity events over the years supporting me. In my early years of leadership, I was told that the more you put into something, the more you will get out of it.”

Peter Thompson, CNZM ‘76

Evan DAVIES, ONZM ‘73

Evan Davies ‘73 was appointed as an Officer of the New Zealand Order of Merit for services to business and governance.

Evan has made a significant contribution to New Zealand’s corporate, public and charitable sectors over more than three decades. As the inaugural Chief Executive Officer of SkyCity Entertainment Group from 1996-2007, he oversaw the conception, licensing, construction, opening and operation of SkyCity Auckland, including the development of the iconic Sky Tower. Under his leadership, SkyCity was recognised as Deloitte’s Company of the Year in 2001, following Evan’s selection as Chief Executive of the Year in 2000.

Evan is currently Group Chief Executive Officer of Todd Corporation, one of New Zealand’s largest family-owned companies. He is also the Crown Manager for the new Dunedin Hospital In-patient Building Project, which he began in June 2025.

Appointments have included Chair of the Health Capital Investment Committee, Chair of the Hospital Redevelopment Partnership Group Canterbury, Deputy Chair of the Hawke’s Bay District Health Board during the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, Board Member of Waterfront Auckland and Chair of the Tāmaki Regeneration Company.

Evan has also dedicated many years to community service, serving as a Trustee of the Melanesian Mission Trust Board since 1998 and the Anglican Trust for Women and Children since 2005. He is also a Director of the Auckland City Mission and chairs the Finance Committee of the Auckland Arts Festival.

Throughout his career, Evan has demonstrated an ability to lead complex organisations, navigate significant challenges and create enduring value for the communities and organisations he has served. His breadth of experience across the private, public and community sectors reflects a commitment to responsible leadership and making a meaningful difference in Aotearoa.

Rodney Wong ‘71 was appointed as an Officer of the New Zealand Order of Merit for services to business and the community

Rodney was the co-founder of the Wildbase Recovery Centre, securing funding and helping lead the development of the nationally significant native wildlife hospital and rehabilitation facility. As a Trustee of the Palmerston North Hospital Foundation, he supported the funding and development of a children’s emergency treatment room, while his leadership of the charitable social service agency Manchester House strengthened support for vulnerable members of the community.

Rodney was Chief Executive of International Fine Foods for a decade and previously owned and led Yoplait New Zealand. He chaired the New Zealand Institute for Crop and Food Research, guiding its merger to form Plant & Food Research, and has held national leadership roles in the promotion of Chinese New Zealand heritage. He has also served as District Governor, reflecting a lifelong commitment to service and leadership and is a Distinguished Fellow of the New Zealand Institute of Directors.

Professor Richard Douglas ‘75 was appointed as a Member of the New Zealand Order of Merit for services to rhinology.

An internationally recognised rhinologist, ENT surgeon and academic, Richard is a former Head of the Department of Surgery at the University of Auckland. He established a leading research programme in rhinology and skull-base surgery, supervising numerous doctoral candidates and helping strengthen New Zealand’s international reputation in academic surgery. He continues to lead an active research group and directs the rhinology clinical fellowship programme at Auckland Hospital. His research into bacteria and mucosal immunity in chronic rhinosinusitis has resulted in more than 200 publications.

Richard performs advanced sinus and skull-base surgery at Auckland City and Gillies Hospitals. A former President of the Australia and New Zealand Rhinologic Society, he also foundedthe Tarāpunga Charitable Trust, which donated a mobile ENT clinic to improve access to specialist healthcare for remote communities across Te Tai Tokerau.
