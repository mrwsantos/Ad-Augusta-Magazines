Shakeel lala ‘09

Since Shakeel Lala ‘09 and his business partners Hardy Michel and Ben Robertson co-founded their company Marloo in 2024, life has been busy. Although their head office is in Wellington, the company’s focus has firmly been on global growth, helped along by a capital raise of over US$12 million in the past twelve months.

Shakeel and his co-founders had previously helped scale retail investing platform Sharesies. Shakeel says it was their time there that made them realise there was an opportunity to help everyday people access financial advice.

Financial advisors, he realised, usually only deal with clients who have considerable assets, given the advisors also have limited time in any one week. Shakeel is keen to see expert financial advice available to a wider base, so a business proposition was born. “Our focus is to increase access for people to financial advisors, by freeing up the advisor’s time to see more people. With many advisors reporting that up to 80% of their time is spent on administration and regulatory work, Marloo’s AI software can take that burden away and vastly speed up the process.” Shakeel says the focus is on allowing advisors to do what they do best, “which is to advise clients, and get AI to sort out the necessary rigorous administrative work associated with providing their appropriate financial advice.”

Shakeel will move to the United States in late 2026 to make inroads into the U.S. market. “The world of AI is fast-moving and global, so that means we are a fast-moving, global company too.” Between offshore visits, Shakeel popped back into Grammar, weaving through the sea of blue uniforms to the familiar Great Hall to see his name on the 2013 Honours Board. It is a building, he reflects, that never changes, “except that the Honours Boards just keep growing around the sides of it.”

Turning 100 years old deserves recognition from your family and friends, and for Old Boys of Auckland Grammar School, from your alma mater as well. The School recently capped two centurions - James (Jim) Espie ‘38 and Thomas (Tom) Doo ‘39 - with a hat that has their name and peer year embroidered on the side with ‘100 Not Out’ added on the back.

The event took place at the annual Augusta Fellowship Luncheon in mid-September. Tom turned 100 in October 2025, and 101-year-old Jim made the milestone back in December 2024. John Kernohan ‘39, who turned 100 in February 2026, was sadly unable to make the event due to ill health.

Other Old Boys from out of Auckland who received their caps are Bruce Leighton ‘39 in Ashburton and Lawrie Hampton ’39 in Hamilton. Bruce turned 100 in May this year and Lawrie turned 100 in October, just prior to this edition going to print.

At the luncheon, Jim and Tom reminisced about their time at Grammar, during World War II. Jim has vivid memories of Headmaster Colin Littlejohn reading out the names of Old Boys who had been killed in the war. Tom recalled the rationing of petrol and food and how boys left school to voluntarily join the Army, Navy, and Air Force.

The war ended before Jim would have been ‘called up’ and by that time, he was employed by The Fletcher Construction Co. Ltd, a company he would have a 27-year association with. When he started in mid-1941, the company was involved with wartime construction projects. Jim started as a cadet in their drawing office in Auckland and finished back in Auckland as Managing Director of Fletchers in 1968. In between times he worked for the company in both Apia and Sydney.

When Tom left school at the end of 1943, he joined the family business that imported manchester, textiles, and later fireworks. After the war, he enrolled at night school to study accounting and built up the business with his three brothers, retiring in 1976. His time was then spent fundraising to build the Chinese Community Centre in Mangere Bridge and Boystown in the city. His work in this area was acknowledged with a QSM for services to the community in 2002.

When Lawrie left school, he became a teacher and taught in primary schools until 1951 before being ordained in the Ministry in 1954. Lawrie worked in four different parishes from Northland to Southland. His final parish was St Andrew’s in Hamilton from which he retired in 1991. Lawrie recalls his Grammar days with pleasure. “I was proud of being a Grammar boy and look back with gratitude for so much that the School, its staff and fellow pupils, offered that enriched my life.” His sentiments are echoed by his fellow centenarians.

All men are thrilled with their caps. John saw an Old Boy capped about a decade ago and never thought he would also have his own one day. “It means a lot to me to have this cap with my name embroidered on it. We are a multi-generational Grammar family and it’s a nice piece of memorabilia to have for us all.” He was delighted to have a personal visit from Old Boys’ Association President Chris Sixton ‘86 who presented him with his cap in his home - and was proudly wearing an old Grammar sweater!
