Just prior to the end of Term 2, Auckland Grammar School hosted its annual Matariki celebration. Matariki is the Māori name for the Pleiades star cluster that rises on the right side of Rangitoto in the Waitematā during winter. Matariki (Māori New Year) is a time to honour ancestors who came before, celebrate the present, and welcome the future alongside family and friends.

Led by senior Te Reo Māori students, the celebrations took place in Te Ara Mātauranga, with the playing of traditional Māori instruments and a special karakia delivered by Form 6 student Lincoln Evans. Whānau and friends who attended were able to develop and strengthen their understanding of this process.

Above left: Tibbs House Head Prefect Ryland Te Tamaki. Above middle: Form 7 student Victah-Levi Hopkinson explains the importance of Matariki, the Pleiades star cluster. Above right: Head of Faculty - Languages, Matua Carlin Shaw, performs during the Matariki celebrations.

Prefects Marlee Jacobs, Ryland Te Tamaki and Nīkau-Te Ha Thorpe and Form 7 student Victah-Levi Hopkinson delivered a presentation that identified the various sections involved in acknowledging Matariki. Information was also shared about how Matariki is celebrated in other islands such as Tonga and Sāmoa.

Guests reflected on the past and remembered friends and family who had passed on during the year. Whānau then experienced how the stars were utilised to make plans for the future, including Hiwa-i-te-rangi, the youngest of all the stars in the cluster, which is associated with dreams and aspirations. Following this, senior students invited everyone to the Heritage Room for refreshments.

Interact, the school-based branch of Rotary, has run at Auckland Grammar School for the past 15 years. Throughout the year, members take part in a number of projects, including restoring Windmill Park through litter collection, weeding and native planting, making survival blankets for people sleeping rough out of clean chip packets, assisting at the Ngatira Tennis Centre with blind, low-vision and ASD kids, and knitting Peggy squares to be made into blankets for The Premmie Knitting Club, which are then distributed to families with premature babies.

In Term 1, the group collected books for the Story Store and raised funds for the Cancer Society through the seven-hour Relay for Life, while activities in Term 2 focused on collections of food and clothing for the Auckland City Mission’s health centre and kitchen.

Term 3 is the busiest term for the group. In early August, students participated in a two-day donation drive for NZ Blood, a quiz night with students from Epsom Girls’ Grammar School raising money for Beyond Water Global, the annual Daffodil Day appeal for the Cancer Society, and the Christmas Book Appeal donating new books to Kiwi Christmas Books for families in need.

Each year, the Minister’s Plate Music Competition brings together Auckland Grammar School’s most accomplished solo musicians for an evening of outstanding performance. The tradition dates back to 1984, when the late Merv Wellington, then Minister of Education, presented the School with a silver plate to mark the opening of the Music Suite. The Amos family followed two years later with a matching plate, giving rise to the Junior Music Plate.

With adjudication provided by Huw Dann, Section Principal Trumpet of the Auckland Philharmonia, the Junior Music Plate was awarded to Form 4 student Ryan Yeh (piano) for his performance of Rachmaninov’s transcription of Kreisler’s Liebesleid, which the adjudicator praised as a “very clear, strong musical voice and a mature sense of space.” Ryan has achieved numerous awards nationally and internationally in both piano and cello.

The Minister’s Plate winner was Form 5 student Ethan Wang (piano), whose rendition of Chopin’s Ballade No. 3 in A-flat Major was called a “musically mature performance, delivered with flair, poise and a great musical personality.” Runner-up honours went to Form 7 student Hanzhao Chen (flute), who tackled the demanding Presto Energico from Liebermann’s Sonata for Flute and Piano.

In early August, six Music groups competed at the annual KBB Music Festival at the Holy Trinity Cathedral and St Mary’s Church.

Concertante, the School’s string ensemble established in 2024, continues to grow in both size and musical ability. This year marked the second year the group was competing and they tackled repertoire spanning the Classical and Romantic periods through to film music, offering valuable performance experience for our younger and developing musicians.

Grammar Virtuosi delivered a challenging programme with impressive technical skill, musical understanding and ensemble awareness, earning a Silver Award.

The Wind Band brought energy and confidence to four entertainingworks, also earning a Silver Award. This is a strong result that reflects the ensemble’s growing musicianship and the progress made throughout the year.

The Big Band followed up their Gold Award at the Auckland Secondary Schools Jazz Competition the previous week with another accomplished performance, receiving a Silver Award. With Elliot Gray at the helm for the first time, these results are a particularly encouraging start.

The Symphony Orchestra gave a polished, well-prepared account of three demanding works, with the group receiving a Silver Award. It is a fitting reflection of the orchestra’s ongoing development and the students’ dedication.

The Concert Band (pictured below) impressed with a varied programme spanning multiple styles, showing maturity, technical control and a strong sense of ensemble. The group received a Gold Award, and they were also selected to perform at the Gala Concert; a significant achievement for the group.

Five students were also chosen to play in the Honours Orchestra, playing with other students alongside two highly respected music educators - Hanzhao Chen (flute), Kelvin Chen (viola), Ben Liu(cor anglais and oboe), Ryan Wang (clarinet) and Daniel Yu (cello).

Congratulations to all musicians on their preparation and commitment. These results reflect the level of excellence and the high standard of musicianship within the Music Department.

KBB MUSIC FESTIVAL

nzcba national festival

Just prior to the end of Term 2, the School’s Concert Band and Wind Band competed at the annual New Zealand Concert Band National Festival in Cambridge. Both groups gave outstanding performances, with each earning a well-deserved Silver Award.

The Concert Band performed a varied programme of Persis (James L. Hosay), Solas Ané (Samuel R. Hazo) and Cajun Folk Songs (Frank Ticheli). Their performance demonstrated strong technical control and musical contrast, earning praise from the adjudicators. The Wind Band made its debut at the festival playing four pieces: Joy (Frank Ticheli), Friday the 13th (Thomas Doss), A Song of Hope (James Swearingen) and Abracadabra (Frank Ticheli). The ensemble also received the Style Award for Excellent Performance of an Entertaining Work.
