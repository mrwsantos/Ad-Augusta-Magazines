upper north island cadenza

After success at the Big Sing Regional Competition, the School’s auditioned choir, Grammarphonics, competed at the Upper North Island Cadenza in August, held at the Sir Howard Morrison Centre in Rotorua. Cadenza is a competitive, finale-style event covering two days of competition with 24 choirs from 12 different schools.

The festival was a valuable experience for the ensemble, providing an opportunity to perform, learn and reflect on their strengths and areas for development. Grammarphonics performed well across the two days of competition and also joined forces with the other participating schools to form a mass choir.

The competition concluded with Grammarphonics receiving a Silver Award; a very pleasing result that reflects the students’ hard work and commitment throughout the year.

winter concert series

The School’s annual Winter Concert Series once again showcased the talent and dedication of Auckland Grammar School’s young musicians with more than 240 students performing in a wide range of ensembles, presenting a programme spanning classical masterworks, jazz, contemporary repertoire and traditional music.

Grammar Virtuosi opened Winter Concert I, whose performances of John Ritchie’s Aquarius, Tchaikovsky’s Serenade for Strings and Vivaldi’s Winter from The Four Seasons displayed outstanding technical skill and musical expression. The Jazz Combo had a three-piece set featuring Chick’s Pain, Miles Davis’ Nardis and Duke Ellington’s Caravan, demonstrating confident improvisation and impressive ensemble playing.

Grammar Voices, the School’s all-comers choir, has continued to flourish as a large and enthusiastic choral ensemble. Their programme included the Samoan folk song Lota Nu’u, Ubi Caritas by Ola Gjeilo and the uplifting spiritual Wade in the Water. Chamber musicians followed with David Hamilton’s Carousel for saxophones and piano, before Grammarphonics presented Scarborough Fair and Northern Lights, highlighting the versatility and musical maturity of our choral and chamber ensembles.

The evening concluded with the Big Band, whose energetic renditions of Gordon Goodwin’s Jazz Police, Miles Davis’ Four and Jimmy Webb’s MacArthur Park brought the audience to its feet.

Winter Concert II celebrated the breadth and diversity of the School’s larger Music groups. The Pipe Band played a traditionalScottish repertoire which created an exciting opening.Concertante, the School’s intermediate string ensemble, performed works by Edward Elgar and Karl Jenkins with poise, while the flute, oboe and clarinet trio presented engaging performances of works by Malcolm Arnold and Paul de Wailly.

The Symphony Orchestra continued the programme with Douglas Lilburn’s Aotearoa Overture and the dramatic The Hut on Hen’s Legs (Baba Yaga) from Mussorgsky’s Pictures at an Exhibition, bringing energy and grandeur to the stage. The Concert Band then delivered a powerful performance of James Hosay’s Persis and Samuel Hazo’s Solas Ane, providing a spectacular finale to two memorable evenings of music.

auckland jazz competition

The School’s Big Band and Jazz Combo competed in the Auckland Jazz Competition held at the Birkenhead RSA in August.

The Jazz Combo opened their three-piece set with Caravan, featuring a tenor saxophone solo from Henry Wong on tenor saxophone. The modal jazz classic Nardis followed, highlighting subtle dynamics and intricate interaction, featuring standout solo work from Henry Wong and Joshua Benson on bass guitar, before their final piece Chick’s Pain closed their set with tightness and driving rhythmic momentum, earning them a Gold Award.

The Big Band presented a programme that showcased both section cohesion and individual solo talent across multiple jazz styles. The group’s set included the Miles Davis classic Four, the smooth, relaxed ballad Li’l Darlin’ by Neal Hefti and finished with a powerful rendition of Jazz Police. The band was also presented with a Gold Award for their high-level musicianship, ensemble precision, and dynamic solo performances.

In late July, students from Auckland Grammar School and Epsom Girls’ Grammar School held their annual Chinese Extravaganza at the Raye Freedman Arts Centre. The student-led event was an incredible success, selling over 700 tickets across three shows. This ensured a sold-out venue which reflected the strong sense of cultural pride and support from both school communities.

Over 170 students worked for months to put the show together. Kicking off with an energetic Lion and Dragon dance to symbolise good fortune and prosperity, the Traditional Dance group performed elegant fan and umbrella pieces that balanced seamlessly with dynamic routines from the Modern Dance team. Musical items by the Chamber group and Mixed Choir delivered rich melodies, complementing a light-hearted Chinese Drama performance and the impressive, high-energy tricks of the Diabolo group.

The success of the production relied heavily on the tireless behind-the-scenes efforts of the Media group, who produced an engaging short film to open each show, and the Backstage crew, who ensured a seamless program throughout. The group extends their thanks to everyone whose hard work made this performance possible, including the Masters in Charge Ellen Peacock and James Yeh for giving up their time to supervise rehearsals and show nights. They are looking forward to continuing this legacy in 2027.

korean Cultural evening

The Korean Cultural Group delivered another stellar Korean Night in June, playing to sold-out crowds in the Centennial Theatre.

The production began early in Term 1 with a strong turnout at auditions, bringing together over 100 students from Auckland Grammar School and Epsom Girls’ Grammar School. Student leaders, led by Kihwan Lee and Seungmin Lee, crafted every element of the show themselves, from choreography and scripts to design and props. The result was a professional, high-energy production that lived up to its reputation as one of the school’s most anticipated events, with queues forming well before doors opened.

The programme spanned the breadth of Korean culture: melodramatic K-Drama scenes, powerful Taekwondo demonstrations, the traditional Fan Dance, soulful K-Band performances, and edgy K-Rap. The Korean Modern Dancers brought the house down while the thunderous Nanta drummers (having performed in assembly and the School’s inaugural Lunar New Year event) were once again a highlight for the audience.

The group would like to thank Messrs Elliot, Evans, Ioelu, Kim, Lee, and Watt who worked tirelessly behind the scenes to help make the event a reality. Despite past uncertainty over enrolments, students’ passionate commitment has kept the tradition alive. With growing global interest in Korean culture, the group looks set to keep flourishing as a treasured part of Grammar life.

sri lankan cultural group

The Sri Lankan Cultural group met on a regular basis throughout the 2026 academic year, welcoming more students and strengthening their connection to the School. In Term 1, members joined the Sri Lankan group from Epsom Girls’ Grammar School for their annual Fia Fia Night, with senior students Isuru Dharmasekara, Menul Rupasingha, Muhandiramge Rodrigo and Evin Senevirathne praised for their dedication and efforts with practices.

The group also enjoyed Cricket together on weekends and is planning a Buddhist temple visit, a community project, and a field trip to celebrate their dual Sri Lankan-New Zealand heritage.
