Landfall Tauraka Young Writers’ Essay Competition

Form 7 student Uzair Khan has been announced as the 2026 Landfall Tauraka Young Writers’ Essay competition winner for his essay entitled The Bright Ones. The prestigious national competition recognises outstanding young essayists from across New Zealand, and the winning essay gets published in Landfall Tauraka, the country’s longest-running literary journal.

Written during Uzair’s final year at Auckland Grammar School, The Bright Ones explores the experience of being an ambitious young New Zealander, reflecting on the tension between pursuing opportunities overseas and remaining connected to home. Uzair explained that the essay was inspired by the familial conversations he has had about his future. “I wanted to write about that pattern, and also about the fact that the education system I’m in was designed somewhere else and seems to lead somewhere else.”

Reflecting on the writing process, Uzair knew what he wanted to write about. “I knew I wanted to write about my circumstances, but I didn’t want it to be a complaint. The hardest part was finding the right balance between honesty about what frustrated me and honesty about what I love.”

North Island Brain Bee Challenge

Throughout the year, Proceres Grammatici - the School’s scholars’ group - have the privilege of being able to visit various institutions and meet with several pioneers in their field.

In August, the group took a visit to the Bioengineering Institute at the University of Auckland, a world-leading research centre that combines engineering, medicine and computer science to improve healthcare through innovative technologies. Its researchers develop advanced computer models of the human body, medical devices and artificial intelligence tools to better diagnose, treat and prevent disease. Working closely with hospitals and industry, the Institute translates research into practical healthcare solutions that improve patient outcomes in New Zealand and around the world.

The visit offered students the chance to interact with post-graduate academics, explore their ongoing projects, and to appreciate the critical role of research in tertiary education. Students engaged with fellow young scientists, gained insight into diverse, cutting-edge projects - such as needleless syringes and computerised heart models - and learned how these innovations are marketed and monetised in a competitive global environment.

Ultimately, this trip proved exceptionally valuable for members of Proceres Grammatici, many of whom plan to attend the University of Auckland next year to begin their tertiary studies.

proceres grammatici

Towards the end of Term 2, three Form 5 students represented Auckland Grammar School at the North Island Brain Bee Challenge, held at the University of Auckland’s Centre for Brain Research.

The annual neuroscience competition brought together more than 140 of the North Island’s highest-achieving Year 11 students, who had qualified through earlier rounds of school-based quizzes.

In the team competition, Auckland Grammar School finished in second place, behind winners Epsom Girls’ Grammar School and a composite team of students from Rototuna High School and Rosmini College coming in third place.

In the individual competition, all three Auckland Grammar School students progressed to the live final. In doing so, Robert Baker, Suryaansh Reddy Bathula and Safwen Oumer were all awarded Neurological Foundation Brain Bee Internships. The internships provide students with the opportunity to spend a day working alongside researchers at the Centre for Brain Research during the Term 3 school holidays in September.

In addition to the competition, students were given the opportunity to tour research laboratories, take part in hands-on neuroscience activities and met researchers working to understand and treat neurological conditions.

internationaleconomics olympiad

Form 7 student Toby King at the International Economics Olympiad.

Founded in 2018, the International Economics Olympiad is an annual global competition in Economics, Business and Finance for high school students. The 2026 tournament was held in Shenzhen, China, and after a rigorous testing process, Form 7 student Toby King was selected as captain of the New Zealand team.

The international competition tested both the team’s theoretical knowledge and practical stamina. It began with a multiple-choice paper covering economic theory and applied Mathematics, followed by five ‘deep-dive’ open-response questions.

The most gruelling aspect of the International Economics Olympiad was the 24-hour business case. Starting at 6:00am, the team had just one day to develop a solution to a real-world economic issue facing New Zealand. This required an 18-hour continuous work stretch to prepare a presentation for a panel of industry judges.

The New Zealand team achieved a strong result, placing in the top 20 teams overall. Individually, Toby’s performance was outstanding. He finished with the top individual score of the New Zealand team and was awarded a Silver medal.

international youngphysicists’ tournament

Form 7 student Zihan Mei (far right) with the New Zealand IYPT team.

The 39th International Young Physicists’ Tournament (IYPT) was held in Zurich, Switzerland in early July. The venue this year was the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology, where Albert Einstein had studied Physics and later lectured as a professor.

IYPT requires students to use the scientific method to conduct research on open-ended problems to obtain precise results. This is followed by an oral defence of their proposed solution in a debate called a ‘Physics Fight’.

Auckland Grammar students have proudly represented New Zealand in IYPT teams for 20 years and Form 7 student Zihan Mei was selected as a member of the New Zealand team this year. 35 countries participated in the tournament this year, with the New Zealand team finishing ninth overall and receiving a Silver medal.

The 2027 IYPT Tournament will take place at the University of Auckland and they are looking forward to hosting the teams of talented young physicists from around the world. A special mention must go to Head of Physics Gavin Jennings for his work with the New Zealand Young Physicists’ team.
