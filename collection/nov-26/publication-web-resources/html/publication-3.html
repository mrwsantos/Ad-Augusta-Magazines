NOVEMBER 2026

Report From the Board

The School roll continues to grow throughout the year, and projections indicate sustained demand for places at Auckland Grammar School over the next few years. As a result, the School has not accepted students through the General Ballot for 2027.

The growth of the School roll remains a key strategic issue for the Board, which is considering ways to respond to increasing demand for a Grammar education without compromising the School’s character or the opportunities available to all students.

The expanding roll has emphasised the importance of our ability to collect the School donation. As the roll increases the School is increasingly reliant on payment of the donation. This enables the School to continue to provide appropriate facilities and resources for students and teachers. For this reason, I would encourage and exhort you to continue to pay the donation. To those that have paid, the Board thanks you for your contribution to the School.

Expanding and enhancing campus facilities remains an ongoing priority: both to accommodate roll growth and to provide high-quality learning environments for students. The Board continues to work with its advisers on the School’s Master Campus Plan, which sets out campus development over the next 25 years and beyond. This includes future planning for the Clive Road/Barnett Road Precinct, covering increased supply of teacher housing, an expanded Tibbs House facility and the expected purchase of 10 Clive Road (you can read more about the project on page 14 of this edition). This work is particularly timely given pressure on the Enrolment Zone and the need to attract teachers while ensuring the facilities at Tibbs House are maintained and enhanced to meet the needs of all boarders.

At this time of year, the Board recognises the outstanding contributions made across the Grammar community. Teaching and non-teaching staff contribute significantly to the School’s curricular and co-curricular life, ensuring students have access to a wide range of opportunities during their time at Grammar. Achievements in academics, sport, the arts, and cultural activities

show that students across all year levels are making the most of the opportunities provided to them.

Now in his fourteenth year at Grammar, the Headmaster and leadership team continue to provide strong, forward-looking leadership. Tim’s ability to attract, retain and motivate high-quality specialist teachers, while ensuring the School is professionally administered, is central to maintaining our high academic, sporting and cultural standards. The Board seeks to recognise and encourage these efforts with the support of the Grammar community. Through your generous contributions during the year, the Board can support and acknowledge our students, teachers and support staff. We thank you for your support.

We are fortunate to enjoy enormous support from Old Boys around the world and the Old Boys in the United Kingdom and United States are particularly strong in their support of the School. As this edition goes to print, the Headmaster and the Director of Advancement Jeff Blackburn ‘67 have attended functions in London and New York where they will meet up with UK and U.S.-based Old Boys. These functions are important as they continue to strengthen the strong relationships between the School and the Old Boys living overseas.

I would also like to congratulate the editorial team - Amanda Booth and Jude Dobson - for their enormous contribution to this redesigned and enlarged edition of Ad Augusta. The magazine provides a superb overview of the many aspects that make up life at Grammar and the contribution of students, teachers, support staff and the wider Grammar community across a multitude of activities and disciplines from music, sport, cultural, academic life and the arts.

Ad Augusta 07

tibbs house and our thanks

Tibbs House and the In Zone boarders are an essential part of Auckland Grammar School, adding a distinctive and highly valued dimension to the wider community. This has been strengthened by the outstanding contribution of the outgoing Director of Boarding, Frank Haszard ’93, who finishes at the end of this year after six years at the helm. An Economics teacher first and foremost, Frank will continue to teach the subject at Grammar.

We are delighted to welcome current Deputy Director of Boarding Matt Cuming into the Director of Boarding role; he will start his tenure at the start of the 2027 academic year. You can read more about both Frank and Matt’s achievements on page 30 of this edition of Ad Augusta.

I also wish to acknowledge the high-quality support we continue to receive from the Foundation Trust and the Headmaster’s Council, ably led by Rob Hamilton ‘81 and Pip Muir respectively, alongside the voluntary contributions of parents and other supporters of the School. Together with the Old Boys’ Association, led by Chris Sixton ‘86 who took up the role as the new President in June, these groups are central to the fabric of Grammar and help make the School distinctive within the state school sector.

Finally, to the students, our best wishes for your forthcoming exams. To the Form 7 leavers, we thank you for your contributions during your time at Grammar. We wish you well as you pursue your careers and look forward to welcoming you back to the School as Old Boys.

Per Angusta Ad Augusta.

Grant Trethewey ‘76Board Chair
