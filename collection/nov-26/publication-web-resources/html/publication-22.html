Volunteering for the School

Giving your time to auckland grammar school.

giving to grammar

Time is a precious commodity, so how you spend it is important. Auckland Grammar School is fortunate to have a sizeable number of parents who chose to spend some of their time volunteering in a chosen activity associated with the School.

For Nicci Magnusson, that is the biennial Art House Tour, taking place on Saturday 21 November 2026. “As a landscape designer, I have a passion for beautiful homes and gardens, so I love the concept. It’s incredibly rewarding to work alongside like-minded parents and be part of something that brings the community together. Supporting local artists, and particularly having our Old Boys exhibit their work, makes it feel even more special.” It is a key fundraiser for Grammar and as Chair she is well involved. However, she says volunteers don’t need to commit huge amounts of time to be involved. “Whether it’s helping at an event, reaching out to potential sponsors, or supporting behind the scenes, there are plenty of ways to contribute. Just find something that works for you.”

For Isabel Mabey, helping with rowing is her focus. “Being involved is a way of supporting my son and showing him that I recognise how important it is to him. It’s also a valuable opportunity to set him the example of giving one’s time and energy to help others. You don’t need any particular skill to volunteer, just enthusiasm, teamwork and a ‘can do’ attitude. I have been everything from cleaner to driver to photographer to cook. We all have life commitments, so it can be an hour, a day, a weekend, whatever works with your own family.” She enjoys seeing the sport up close. “The commitment, dedication and sacrifice of the studentsis inspiring; I feel lucky to have the opportunity to be around that energy.”

Sophie Anderson is Chair of the Gala Dinner Committee. The mid-year event is an annual fundraising event that, like the Art House Tour, raises funds for the Academic Endowment Fund. This fund is in place to help recruit, reward and retain excellent teachers for the School and is something Sophie is more than happy to fundraise for. “My two sons have benefitted hugely from the fantastic teaching staff at Grammar, and I am passionate about the Endowment Fund initiative and the School’s ability, as a result of this fund, to reward teachers more generally.” Sophie enjoys the group of people she works with to make the popular event a success. “The Committee consists of an incredibly strong and diversely skilled group of motivated Grammar parents who I feel privileged to have the opportunity to work with and get to know. “

Sophie recommends volunteering to anyone musing on the idea. “It is a wonderful way to feel involved at Auckland Grammar School, to be connected to something bigger than yourself and for me, it has deepened my understanding of the School community and the people who work and volunteer within that environment.”

What if there was a way to donate money to Auckland Grammar School but it did not cost you anything? In fact, it may even save you money. Such is the case with the association between the School and Raizor.

The initiative is called ‘Insurance for Good’ and the name says exactly what it means. For any new business insurance policy taken out with their partners Gallagher Insurance, 15% of the commission is donated to a nonprofit. For the Grammar Community, that nonprofit is the Foundation Trust at the School. The hope is that the wider Grammar community will embrace the concept and support the School through this ongoing, passive giving opportunity.

A handful of businesses have already come on board, donating $7,000 to date with nearly twice that already committed this year. Among them are past parent Richard Miles, founder of 3 Wise Men, who said the programme made supporting the School effortless. “Gallagher made the renewal streamlined and easy. The concept is a win-win for both parties and an excellent way to continue to support the School that had such a positive impact on our son.”

Carmen Platt has also signed on and says it delivers benefits for both the School and her business CFX Signage. “We’ve found Insurance for Good well worth doing. We get to check our insurance is competitively priced, with the bonus of a donation to the School, and it takes next to no time.”

Jared Thomas, co-founder of Raizor, is the connection point between the client and Gallagher. He said finding broker expertise and value for the client is not a difficult task. ”Whatever sector your business is in, chances are that Gallagher already has deep, specialist experience there. As one of New Zealand’s leading insurance brokers, they have spent decades building trusted, industry-specific expertise.”

Gallagher’s specialist areas include construction and development, architects and consultancies, transport and logistics, financial and professional services, manufacturing and commercial property to name a few.

So far, Raizor has donated $625,000 to non-profit organisations nationwide. If the School’s Foundation Trust became a regular entity for donations, Major Gifts and Bequests Manager Daniel Reddish says it would make a huge difference to the School.

“Having a steady stream of income coming in is obviously very helpful. If multiple businesses signed on, even if their annual insurance costs are minimal, the aggregated effect is good news for Grammar, and good news for the business too if they can better manage their risks and market-test their pricing. It’s an easy and passive way to give back and we hope our community supports it.”
