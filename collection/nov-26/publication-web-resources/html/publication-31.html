Over 140 Old Boys gathered at the Grammar TEC Rugby Clubrooms for the second annual Grammar vs King’s Old Boys Luncheon, in honour of the highly anticipated 1st XV Rugby match between Auckland Grammar School and King’s College. Those in attendance heard from a fantastic panel line-up, including Brandon Jackson ‘83, Iain Wood ‘77 and Jeremy Stanley ‘88 alongside father Joe,

with James Gemmell ‘94 back to steer proceedings. The event was a resounding success with a number of guests in attendance already keen to purchase a table for the 2027 Luncheon. The weekend was capped off with an emphatic 52-27 win to Grammar the following day.

The annual Golf Evening was held at Remuera Driving Range in early August, bringing together 30 Old Boys for an enjoyable evening of golf, connection, and friendly competition. The turnout was strong with a range of ages and handicaps, and the evening carried the right balance of tradition, humour, and competitive spirit that makes these gatherings so worthwhile.

The golf itself produced some impressive moments under pressure. The closest to the pin competition was tightly contested, with Peter Liggins ‘84 and Bryan Screen ‘88 getting within 1m of the pin - a strong effort in front of a vocal group of Old Boys, and a fair reminder that there is still plenty of talent among the ranks.

The ‘Longest Drive’ competition also drew plenty of attention, withthe winning drive from Paul Thomson ‘88 measuring bang on 250m. A satisfactory effort under pressure, and one that earned suitable respect from the group, along with the usual level of good natured commentary. Overall, the evening was a great success. It was well attended, well spirited, and a valuable opportunity to keep the Auckland Grammar Old Boys network connected. Thank you to everyone who attended and helped make the evening enjoyable.

annual old boys’ golf evening

The UK Friends of Auckland Grammar and the UK members of the King’s College Old Collegians Association joined together on a glorious Sunday in June to compete for the Buchanan-Myers Cup. This annual touch rugby fixture is named for two distinguished alumni of the respective schools who, like the players, spent much time in the United Kingdom after graduating.

The game was fast paced, with King’s getting out of the gates quickly. From there, despite valiant efforts in both attack and defence, the ability of the King’s Old Collegians to spread the ball beyond the outer reaches of the tired Grammar defence sealed the win for them. After the match, the teams enjoyed reminiscing over times at school together at the after match function. The connection between Grammar and King’s, our shared history of almost 130 years and 215 games played, was profoundly felt.

King’s College thus retained the Buchanan-Myers Cup for the fifth straight year. The UK Friends of Grammar would like to thank all players and spectators for another wonderful game and look forward to challenging King’s College again next year.
