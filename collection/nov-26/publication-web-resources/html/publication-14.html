student stories

Form 7 students Jackson Couper, Lawson Pryor and Taye Thomson were named as members of the Junior Tall Blacks, New Zealand’s Under 17 boys’ basketball team. They travelled to Istanbul to compete at the FIBA Under 17 World Championships in late June, New Zealand’s fourth World Cup appearance, following an historic fourth-place finish from the 2024 tournament.

Drawn into a difficult group with the host nation Türkiye, Puerto Rico and Slovenia, the Kiwis showed from game one that they belonged. The team played Türkiye on the opening day of the tournament, where the team had a 25-point lead going into the third quarter. Unfortunately, the team eventually lost 100-112 in a double-overtime battle. Their second game against Puerto Rico was unfortunately much the same, with the team being 13 points ahead before a tough 77-85 loss.

The team’s final pool play game against Slovenia was a must-win if they wanted to advance to the Round of 16. It was another very close game, but the team came away with their first win of the tournament (79-74). The team then played four further games in the classification rounds, recording losses against Serbia (91-121), China (79-92) and Cote d’Ivoire (81-85) as well as 104-83 win over Japan. These results meant the New Zealand team finished 12th place overall out of 16 teams.

The 2026 FIBA Under 17 World Cup was the conclusion of a three-year journey for Jackson, Lawson and Taye. Jackson is a steady facilitator, defender and 3-point shooter. Lawson is a sharp-shooting guard who showed his scoring ability with several double-digit outings, while Taye is a reliable and physical defender and balances his Basketball responsibilities alongside those of Deputy Head Prefect. All three students are members of the School’s Premier Basketball team and have been a part of New Zealand’s junior pathway since 2024, when they qualified at the Oceania tournament for the Asia Cup in 2025. Their top four finish was enough for them to qualify for the 2026 World Cup.

new zealand representatives

At Auckland Grammar School, the New Zealand Representative badge, along with Colours, is one of the highest sporting honours a student can receive. In 2026 to date, New Zealand Representative badges have been awarded to 19 students across a range of sporting codes, including Archery, Athletics, Cycling, Orienteering, Open Water Swimming, Rowing and Softball.

Form 6 student Ethan Mora represented New Zealand at the Oceania Athletics Championships in Darwin in May. Competing in the 3000m, the race started fast, with only three athletes, including Ethan, breaking from the rest of the field. The race then became tactical, with the chasing group too far back to catch up. In the final kilometre, the pace progressively increased, with Ethan making his move with 300m to go. He held on to win the Oceania title by only 28 milliseconds in a time of 8:40.88.

Form 6 student Max Allan represented New Zealand at the Tour de l’Abitibi, an International junior Cycling stage race held annually in Quebec, Canada. As part of the prestigious UCI Junior Nations Cup series, the tour consisted of seven stages totalling 708km across six days. Max competed against national teams from across the world, finishing 13th on General Classification and seventh on Youth General Classification in a field of over 80 riders. This included three top-25 stage finishes over the week.

In July, three senior students represented New Zealand sailing at the 29er World Championships in Kiel, Germany. Form 5 student Matteo Barker, Form 6 student Hugo Smith and Form 7 student Oliver Stone took part in the six-day regatta across the Baltic Sea which featured a range of conditions, with mainly tough offshore winds that proved challenging for everyone. With a record-breaking fleet of 276 boats from 35 countries, New Zealand won the Nations Cup as the best overall nation.

The New Zealand Representative badge is not simply celebrating selection; it is an affirmation of ‘The Grammar Way’ - achievement built on humility, hard work, and loyalty to team and School.

student stories

Learning doesn’t always happen in the classroom. In 2026, 33 Auckland Grammar School students took part in the Gateway programme, stepping into workplaces across a wide range of industries including electrical, freight and logistics, construction, laboratory science, aviation, dairy farming and customer service.

Gateway continued to grow this year, with new employer connections including Lazer Plumbing Grey Lynn, Bracewell Construction (above left), Leck Electrical and Commuto Construction. The School was also fortunate to have the continued support of returning employers including Mainfreight, LabPlus, Avanti Automotive, the Auckland Aero Club (above right), Grammar Electrical and Wrightson Construction.

Two highlights this year were our new connections with Winstone Aggregates and Southern Cross Surgical Centre. These placements gave students the opportunity to experience specialised workplaces and see first-hand what working in these industries is really like.

The value of Gateway was clear in the feedback from students. They gained a better understanding of the workplace, developed practical skills and learnt more about the career pathways they are considering. For some, the experience confirmed that they were heading in the right direction. For others, it showed them that a particular career wasn’t for them. Knowing what you don’t want to do can be just as useful when making decisions about the future. Students also valued the chance to meet people working in their chosen industries, build their confidence and experience the expectations of a real workplace.

A huge thank you goes to the Gateway employers. Giving up time to welcome a student into the workplace, sharing knowledge and providing an honest insight into an industry makes a real difference. These experiences give the students a chance to see what lies beyond school and begin making connections between their interests, their strengths and their future.

headmaster’s heritage committee

Led by Masters in Charge Amanda Booth and James Floerchinger,the Headmaster’s Heritage Committee comprises a small group of Form 6 and 7 students, whose aim is to document the forming legacies of current students, how they have made an impact on the Grammar community and how they will in the future too, as Old Boys of Auckland Grammar School. The committee looks to uncover the stories of the men whose names appear on the Honours Board in the Great Hall and of those others who have lived interesting and adventurous lives.

In 2026, the committee was tasked with a project in the form of ‘shadowbox presentations’ - posters that show the depth and breadth of prominent Old Boys. Several posters have already been created and printed, including Cyril Bassett (class of 1905), Sir Edmund Hillary ‘31, Hamish Kerr ‘10 and Sir Leslie Munro (class of 1915). The group worked together to come up with a list of other Old Boys to research, with the Headmaster issuing a challenge to the group to produce a poster for each Main Block classroom.

Student leader Rupert Glaetzer is enjoying the opportunity to learnmore about the history of the School and the Old Boys that have walked the halls. “Leading the Heritage Committee has been one of the most rewarding opportunities for me this year. It feels so special to be deeply in touch with the legacy of the School, in ways of preservation of history and acknowledgement of the past. Students will see these Grammar men on the walls of classrooms and feel proud to be a member of our School. These men will be an inspiration for them; those who were once students like us, who have done extraordinary things in the world just like we can.”

The Headmaster’s Heritage Committee is dedicated to capturing achievements of current students and Old Boys of all ages. With more than 2,800 current students and a vast network of Old Boys that grows each year, there is much to document, and the committee is looking forward to uncovering these stories in 2027, doing its part to preserve the history of the School.
